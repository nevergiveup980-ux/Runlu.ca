## Purpose

T01 does not ask whether the Yijing can simply “tell the future.” It first asks a more basic question:

**What kind of thinking system is preserved in the received Yijing, and which parts are useful for GUANSHI as a disciplined language of change?**

The working answer is organized through nine keys:

**经 → 传 → 卦 → 爻 → 象 → 数 → 变 → 时 → 位**

These nine keys are not nine independent doctrines. They separate textual layers, representational devices and decision concepts that are often blended together in popular explanations.

---

# 1. 经 · Jing — the core textual layer

For GUANSHI, “经” refers primarily to the core Zhouyi material: the 64 hexagram figures/names, hexagram statements and line statements.

Modern scholarship treats the received Yijing as a composite text rather than assuming that every part was written at one moment or by one author. Traditional attributions to Fu Xi, King Wen, the Duke of Zhou and Confucius are historically important traditions, but they are not automatically treated as established authorship claims.

### GUANSHI use
When citing a rule, first ask:
- Is it actually in the core Zhouyi?
- Is it in a later canonical commentary?
- Is it a still later interpretive school?

This prevents later metaphysical systems from being silently projected backward into the oldest textual layer.

**Label:** Historical text + Traditional interpretation.

---

# 2. 传 · Zhuan — the commentarial/philosophical layer

The received Yijing includes later commentarial writings traditionally grouped as the “Ten Wings.” Modern reference works commonly distinguish these from the older core text and place their composition in later centuries.

The commentarial layer greatly expands the philosophical vocabulary of change, cosmology, images, line relations, timing and human action.

### GUANSHI use
The commentaries are not discarded. Instead, they are labeled.

A sentence from `繫辭 / Xi Ci`, `彖傳 / Tuan`, `象傳 / Xiang`, `說卦 / Shuo Gua`, etc. should be identified as a commentarial statement rather than presented as if it were necessarily part of the earliest Zhouyi core.

**Label:** Canonical traditional commentary + textual-history caution.

---

# 3. 卦 · Gua — a state configuration

A hexagram is a six-line configuration. GUANSHI treats it first as a compact representation of a **state or situation structure**, not as a magical object.

Useful questions include:
- What is the current configuration?
- Which relations are stable?
- Which are under tension?
- Which parts are changing?
- What happens if one local condition changes?

### Boundary
A hexagram can organize attention and comparison. Its historical use in divination does not by itself establish modern predictive accuracy.

**Label:** Traditional + Symbolic representation.

---

# 4. 爻 · Yao — local variable and position

Each hexagram has six lines, conventionally read from bottom to top. The core Zhouyi has line statements associated with those positions.

Historical scholarship links the development of line statements to early divinatory contexts, while also warning against reading later technical line systems back into the earliest period.

### GUANSHI use
The line is interpreted as a useful abstract idea:

**A system can change locally before the whole situation changes.**

This becomes a practical modeling rule: identify the variable, actor, constraint or relation that can move first.

**Label:** Historical divination structure + GUANSHI abstraction.

---

# 5. 象 · Xiang — image, pattern and analogy

The received Yijing repeatedly uses images and analogical correspondences. `象` therefore becomes a way to compress a complex relation into a memorable pattern.

### GUANSHI use
Images are allowed to generate hypotheses, reframings and comparisons. They are not allowed to bypass evidence.

The rule is:

**象可启发，不可替代事实。**  
An image may suggest; it may not replace observation.

**Label:** Symbolic / heuristic.

---

# 6. 数 · Shu — structure, number and procedure

Numbers have a real place in Yijing traditions and divinatory procedures. Later “象数” traditions develop this much further.

GUANSHI keeps an important boundary:

**Traditional numerical symbolism is not the same thing as modern statistical evidence.**

A number can belong to a traditional procedure while still requiring independent validation before it is treated as predictive.

### GUANSHI use
- preserve reproducible calculation rules;
- separate calculation from interpretation;
- if an output claims measurable predictive value, test it prospectively.

**Label:** Traditional procedure; empirical validity separate.

---

# 7. 变 · Bian — transition

`变` is the center of T01.

The received commentarial tradition repeatedly treats change and transformation as central. `繫辭下` contains the compact sequence:

> 穷则变，变则通，通则久。

GUANSHI does not read this as a supernatural law guaranteeing success. It extracts a disciplined decision principle:

**When the current configuration reaches a constraint, search for what can change; then test whether that change opens a viable path.**

This is different from forcing change for its own sake.

**Label:** Traditional philosophical principle → modern decision heuristic.

---

# 8. 时 · Shi — timing and context

Timing is not an optional decoration. In Yijing commentary, the appropriateness of action depends on the situation and moment. The Tuan commentary on Gen contains the concise formulation:

> 时止则止，时行则行。

GUANSHI converts this into:
- the same action can be good in one phase and poor in another;
- a recommendation must carry a time horizon;
- “wait,” “test,” “advance,” “hold,” and “exit” are different actions;
- an action without a timing condition is incomplete.

**Label:** Traditional timing principle + decision framing.

---

# 9. 位 · Wei — position, role and fit

The Yijing commentarial tradition pays close attention to position: upper/lower, central/non-central, correspondence and the relation between a line and its place.

GUANSHI abstracts `位` as:

**Do not judge an actor or action outside its position in the system.**

Questions:
- What role does this person/variable occupy?
- What resources and constraints come with that position?
- Is the action appropriate to the current role and capacity?
- Would the same behavior mean something different in another position?

This becomes one of GUANSHI’s main defenses against context-free advice.

**Label:** Traditional relational concept → contextual modeling heuristic.

---

# The T01 synthesis

The nine keys can be compressed into one GUANSHI flow:

1. **经 / Source** — What does the earliest relevant text actually say?
2. **传 / Interpretation** — Which later layer supplied the explanation?
3. **卦 / State** — What is the present configuration?
4. **爻 / Variable** — What can move locally?
5. **象 / Pattern** — What analogy helps us see the structure?
6. **数 / Procedure** — What rule or calculation is reproducible?
7. **变 / Transition** — What changes are plausible?
8. **时 / Timing** — When should action change?
9. **位 / Position** — Who/what is acting from which role and constraint set?

Then GUANSHI adds the modern loop:

**Observe → Frame → Estimate → Decide → Record → Review → Reweight**

The traditional layer therefore contributes **questions and representations**; the validation loop determines whether any predictive or practical rule earns weight.

# What T01 keeps / what T01 rejects

## Keep
- change as the central object of attention;
- state and transition thinking;
- position-sensitive interpretation;
- timing-sensitive action;
- local change vs whole-system change;
- image/analogy as hypothesis generation;
- reproducible procedures as objects that can be tested;
- textual layering and source discipline.

## Reject or quarantine
- treating later interpretations as if they were all original Zhouyi doctrine;
- deterministic “this hexagram guarantees X” language;
- post-hoc reinterpretation after the outcome is known;
- substituting symbolic correspondence for physical causation;
- treating traditional number symbolism as modern probability/statistics;
- using vague language that can explain every possible outcome.

# T01 evidence boundary

The source record can establish:
- what the received texts contain;
- how scholars analyze textual layers and historical divination;
- how later philosophical vocabulary developed.

It cannot by itself establish:
- that hexagrams predict future events above chance;
- that symbolic correspondences are physical causal mechanisms;
- that one traditional interpretation is uniquely correct.

Any such claim enters the GUANSHI prospective testing protocol.
