# RUNLU SHORT FICTION — Story 001 V13 Rebuild Blueprint

**Editorial status:** Development draft. Not a completed story, not approved for publication.

## Working title
**The Things She Didn't Take**

Alternative titles to check before publication: *An Inventory of Leaving*; *The Empty Shelf*. All titles are provisional and require separate collision research.

## Core distinction from V12
Remove the clock, seven-minute timing motif, clockmaker profession, coded messages, and any secret left by a deceased father. Do not adapt the V12 scenes by merely substituting objects. Construct a new plot driven by a consequential present-day choice.

## Logline
When a Calgary woman helps her estranged father downsize from his longtime home to a smaller apartment, their disagreement over what to discard forces her to confront a family history in which both people have mistaken practicality for indifference.

## Characters
- **Claire Bennett**, 49, a project coordinator in Calgary; values efficiency and assumes competence is the same as care.
- **Arthur Bennett**, 76, retired municipal maintenance worker, alive and mentally capable; dislikes explaining himself and refuses to keep possessions merely for sentiment.
- **Ethan**, Claire's teenage son, who is supposed to help with the move but has his own weekend commitment.
- **Margaret**, Claire's mother and Arthur's former spouse, present only through an ordinary telephone conversation; no hidden revelation.

## Narrative engine
Arthur has accepted a smaller apartment and set a firm move-out date. Claire has one Saturday to help clear the garage and kitchen. A reusable shelving unit that Claire wants to save is already promised to a young neighbor starting over after a difficult move. Claire sees a careless giveaway; Arthur sees a useful object serving someone who needs it. Neither is wholly right. The conflict intensifies when the pickup collides with Ethan's school commitment. Claire must choose whether to keep controlling the move or trust other people to carry part of it.

## Ten-scene structure (target 3,500–4,500 original English words)
1. **The List** — Claire arrives with color-coded labels; Arthur has already made his own simpler list.
2. **The Garage** — The two disagree over what counts as worth keeping.
3. **The Promise** — Claire discovers the shelves were offered to a neighbor without consulting her.
4. **The Phone Call** — Ethan needs a ride; Claire refuses, then regrets her tone.
5. **The Kitchen Drawer** — Ordinary household items reveal habits, not secrets; Claire recognizes how little she has asked.
6. **The Pickup** — The neighbor arrives early with a borrowed vehicle; Claire's plan unravels.
7. **The Argument** — Arthur explains why he would rather give something useful away than store it indefinitely. He does not deliver a sentimental monologue.
8. **The Choice** — Claire leaves the garage unfinished to help Ethan, trusting Arthur and the neighbor to finish the loading.
9. **The Apartment** — Later she visits Arthur's smaller place, noticing what he deliberately chose to keep.
10. **The Space** — The final image is an ordinary empty shelf Claire chooses not to fill; emotional resolution through action, not a twist.

## Literary constraints
- Contemporary Alberta details should be natural, not travel-guide exposition.
- Avoid villains, deathbed revelations, hidden letters, miraculous coincidences, and a moral explained in dialogue.
- Give Arthur a credible perspective; Claire's efficiency is also a form of care.
- Maintain an emotionally restrained, observant, English-first narrative voice.
- End with a concrete, modest act rather than a maxim.
- This is a **new story**, not a disguised clock-story rewrite.
