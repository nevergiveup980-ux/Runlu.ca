# RUNLU GUANSHI · 传统原典线 · Traditional Source Spine

Status: **V0.3 · source spine / learning sequence**

Public page: `guanshi-tradition.html`

## Why this line exists

The traditional line is organized for learning and model dependency:

**《易经》 → 阴阳 → 五行 → 八卦 → 干支 → 八字 → 六爻 / 梅花 → 风水**

This arrow is **not** a claim that each item historically descended from the previous item in a simple chronological chain. The systems developed over long periods, often in parallel, and were repeatedly recombined by later traditions.

GUANSHI therefore keeps two maps at once:

1. **Learning sequence** — a practical order for understanding how concepts are reused by later systems.
2. **Historical source map** — the earliest or most influential texts we can responsibly identify, with uncertainty preserved.

## T01 · 《易经》 / Yijing — change, position, relation

### Core contribution
- change is treated as structured rather than random narrative;
- states are represented through line/hexagram structures;
- position, relation, timing and transformation become central questions.

### Primary anchors
- Zhouyi core text;
- `繫辭上 / Xi Ci I`;
- `繫辭下 / Xi Ci II`.

### GUANSHI use
Use the Yijing as a vocabulary for **change, state, transition, timing and relation**, not as automatic proof of future events.

### Evidence label
**Traditional + Symbolic** unless a specific independent empirical claim is being tested.

---

## T02 · 阴阳 / Yin–Yang — relational polarity

### Core contribution
Yin–yang provides a relational language for:
- opposition without absolute separation;
- alternation;
- relative strength/weakness;
- activity/rest;
- expansion/contraction;
- context-dependent balance.

The traditional literature does not require GUANSHI to treat yin and yang as scientifically established physical forces.

### Primary anchor
`繫辭上` contains the well-known formulation linking one yin and one yang with dao and also places strong/weak line relations at the center of change.

### GUANSHI use
Translate yin–yang first into observable contrasts or changing relationships before making any practical recommendation.

### Evidence label
**Symbolic / Traditional**.

---

## T03 · 五行 / Five Phases — cycle and correspondence

### Core contribution
Five Phases organizes relations among water, fire, wood, metal and earth and later becomes a major correspondence system reused across calendrical, medical, cosmological, fate-calculation and environmental traditions.

### Primary anchor
`《尚书·洪范》 / Shangshu · Great Plan` explicitly lists water, fire, wood, metal and earth and assigns characteristic tendencies to them.

### GUANSHI use
Treat Five Phases as a **relationship/cycle model**. Any claim that a phase directly causes a measurable modern outcome must be independently tested.

### Evidence label
**Traditional**; particular causal claims remain separate.

---

## T04 · 八卦 / Bagua — state and pattern classification

### Core contribution
Eight trigrams provide a compact pattern vocabulary. Later traditions map them to directions, family roles, natural images, timing and many other correspondences.

### Primary anchors
- `繫辭上` links the two forms, four images and eight trigrams;
- `說卦 / Shuo Gua` develops trigram images, relations and directional associations.

### GUANSHI use
Use bagua as a structured classification lens. Do not assume that an assigned trigram has proven predictive power.

### Evidence label
**Traditional + Symbolic**.

---

## T05 · 干支 / Heavenly Stems & Earthly Branches — time encoding

### Core contribution
Ten Heavenly Stems and twelve Earthly Branches form repeating calendrical combinations. The sixty-term stem–branch cycle becomes a common timing scaffold reused by many later systems.

### Historical caution
Stem–branch calendrical notation is very old and should not be described as a late invention produced by Bazi or feng shui. Later metaphysical systems attach additional correspondences to an older calendrical framework.

### GUANSHI use
Separate:
1. **calendar/encoding facts**;
2. **traditional correspondences**;
3. **personality or predictive claims**.

Only the first category is factual by default.

### Evidence label
**Established historical/calendar structure + Traditional interpretations**.

---

## T06 · 八字 / Bazi / Four Pillars — person × time pattern model

### Core contribution
Four Pillars uses year, month, day and hour, each represented by a stem–branch pair, yielding eight characters. Later Ziping traditions organize these through day-master relations, Five Phases and additional interpretive rules.

### Source anchors
Historical study should distinguish among:
- early fate-calculation practices;
- Li Xuzhong traditions;
- the later Ziping reorganization;
- `渊海子平 / Yuanhai Ziping`;
- `三命通会 / Sanming Tonghui`;
- later commentarial traditions.

Attributions and textual layers are not always secure and must be recorded as such.

### GUANSHI use
Bazi may generate **testable hypotheses or structured questions**. Deterministic claims are never promoted to fact without prospective validation.

### Evidence label
**Traditional / test required**.

---

## T07 · 六爻 / 梅花 — event-focused divination methods

### Core contribution
These methods move from broad cosmological/classification structures toward concrete question framing and event interpretation.

For GUANSHI they are kept distinct:
- **六爻 / line-based divination** — hexagram and moving-line interpretation with later technical rule systems;
- **梅花 / Meihua** — number/time/image-based methods associated in later tradition with Shao Yong, with textual attribution requiring careful historical review.

### Historical caution
Do not collapse all later technical divination into the ancient Zhouyi itself. The technical systems accumulated across later centuries.

### GUANSHI use
Their first research value is as **structured reframing protocols**. Any predictive claim must be preregistered before the outcome.

### Evidence label
**Traditional + Exploratory**.

---

## T08 · 风水 / Feng Shui — environment, siting and symbolic space

### Core contribution
Feng shui brings the line into physical environment: siting, orientation, terrain, water, movement and spatial organization, together with symbolic/correlative rules.

### Source anchors
- `葬书 / Zangshu` tradition, historically associated with Guo Pu but with attribution/textual-history cautions;
- later form-school and compass-school traditions;
- modern historical studies of Chinese geomancy.

### GUANSHI use
Every feng shui case is split into two layers:

**Measurable environment**
- daylight;
- noise;
- ventilation;
- temperature;
- access;
- layout;
- circulation;
- safety;
- privacy;
- terrain/drainage where relevant.

**Traditional symbolism**
- directional correspondences;
- symbolic forms;
- traditional qi / auspiciousness rules.

The two layers must never be silently blended.

### Evidence label
**Mixed: measurable environmental evidence + Traditional/Symbolic rules**.

# The dependency map

The useful conceptual dependencies are:

- **Yijing** supplies the broad language of change, position and transformation.
- **Yin–Yang** supplies relational polarity.
- **Five Phases** supplies cyclic/correlative relations.
- **Bagua** supplies compact pattern classes and images.
- **Stems/Branches** supply a time-encoding scaffold.
- **Bazi** combines time encoding with yin–yang/five-phase interpretation around a person.
- **Six Lines / Meihua** apply symbolic structures to concrete questions/events.
- **Feng Shui** applies correlative models to physical environment and siting.

This is a **model-dependency map**, not a simple genealogy.

# Source protocol for V0.3+

Every traditional node should eventually have four records:

1. **Primary text** — what an original or canonical text actually says.
2. **Textual history** — date, authorship, layers, later commentary and uncertainty.
3. **Modern scholarly interpretation** — how historians/scholars describe the system.
4. **Empirical claim audit** — if the node makes a measurable claim, what modern evidence says.

A traditional statement is never upgraded merely because it is old, famous or internally coherent.
