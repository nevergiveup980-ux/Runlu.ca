# GUANSHI T06 · Bazi / Four Pillars

Status: **V0.9 · research note**

Public page: `guanshi-bazi.html`

## Working title

**八字 · 人与时间的语法**  
**Bazi · The Grammar of Person × Time**

## Core GUANSHI rule

> A correct chart is not the same thing as a correct life prediction.

**排盘正确，不等于人生预测正确。**

This node deliberately separates five layers that are often blended together:

1. birth facts;
2. calendrical/chart conversion;
3. traditional interpretive model;
4. claims about personality/life events;
5. empirical outcome audit.

A valid earlier layer does not automatically validate a later layer.

---

## 1 · Birth facts

Minimum raw inputs may include:
- date of birth;
- clock time of birth;
- place of birth;
- time zone / daylight-saving convention where relevant;
- declared calendar / solar-term convention used by the charting method.

These inputs are facts or explicit conversion assumptions. They are not themselves fate claims.

Privacy note: date + time + place can identify a person. Public GUANSHI cases should minimize personal data and require consent before exposing any identifiable chart.

---

## 2 · Chart mechanics

Four Pillars converts the birth moment into:

- year pillar = one stem + one branch;
- month pillar = one stem + one branch;
- day pillar = one stem + one branch;
- hour pillar = one stem + one branch.

Four pairs = eight characters / 八字.

Mechanical requirements for GUANSHI:
- calculation rules declared before interpretation;
- same input must reproduce the same chart under the same declared convention;
- disagreement between schools or software must be logged as a conversion/model issue, not hidden;
- an accurate conversion receives credit only for conversion accuracy.

---

## 3 · Historical layering

### Older temporal scaffold
The sexagenary stem–branch calendar predates mature Bazi practice by many centuries.

### Earlier fate-calculation layer
Historical literature associates Li Xuzhong 李虚中 with an earlier fate-calculation tradition. Surviving `Li Xuzhong mingshu` material is textually layered; modern reference scholarship cautions that explicit hour/Four-Pillars material may be later compilation rather than the earliest layer.

### Zi Ping layer
Later tradition associated with Xu Ziping 徐子平 centers the day stem as a primary reference and develops the familiar Four-Pillars interpretive architecture.

GUANSHI does not assume:
- one historical inventor created the modern system in a single step;
- all surviving texts belong to the dates traditionally attributed to them;
- later concepts can be projected unchanged into Tang or earlier practice.

Traditional texts to map:
- `李虚中命书 / Li Xuzhong mingshu`;
- `渊海子平 / Yuanhai Ziping`;
- `三命通会 / Sanming Tonghui`;
- later `滴天髓 / Ditian Sui`;
- later `子平真诠 / Ziping Zhenquan`.

For each, keep authorship, edition, transmission and commentary questions separate.

---

## 4 · Traditional interpretive layer

Common later Bazi components include:
- Day Master / 日主;
- Ten Gods / 十神;
- month command / 月令;
- seasonal strength / 旺衰;
- hidden stems / 藏干;
- combinations, clashes, punishments, harms and related stem–branch relations;
- structure / 格局;
- useful god / 用神;
- luck cycles / 大运;
- annual / periodic overlays.

GUANSHI treats these as **Traditional** model components unless a specific claim has independent evidence.

These categories may be internally coherent without being empirically predictive. Internal coherence and external validity are separate questions.

---

## 5 · Life-claim boundary

The following become empirical claims if presented as factual predictions:
- personality traits;
- relationship outcomes;
- career success;
- wealth;
- health;
- timing of major life events;
- accident, marriage, divorce, promotion, business outcomes;
- lifespan or death claims.

High-stakes deterministic claims are outside GUANSHI's operational use.

For medical, legal, financial, safety or mental-health decisions, symbolic Bazi interpretation never controls the recommendation.

---

## 6 · GUANSHI prospective test architecture

### Question
Does Bazi add useful information beyond ordinary facts already known about the person?

### Model M0 — reality baseline
Use only relevant non-Bazi variables, depending on the outcome:
- age;
- sex where methodologically relevant;
- location;
- occupation;
- education;
- income/resources;
- history;
- behavior;
- measured personality;
- base rates;
- environment;
- current constraints.

### Model M1 — baseline + Bazi
Add a preregistered Bazi feature set under a declared school/rule set.

### Rules
1. Define outcome before reading the chart.
2. Freeze the charting convention and interpretive school.
3. Freeze which Bazi features may be used.
4. Prevent post-outcome switching of school, useful god or explanatory rule.
5. Limit personal clues during blind readings.
6. Score M0 and M1 on cases not used to design the rule.
7. Keep Bazi weight only if M1 shows repeatable incremental value.

If M1 does not outperform M0, Bazi receives **0 predictive weight** for that target, even if retrospective narratives sound persuasive.

---

## 7 · Personality test design

A weak test asks:
> Does this description feel like me?

That is vulnerable to vague statements, confirmation bias and selective memory.

A stronger test:
- preregister 3–5 measurable traits;
- use a standardized questionnaire or behavioral proxy;
- define scoring in advance;
- generate Bazi classification without seeing questionnaire answers;
- compare accuracy against base-rate and simple demographic baselines;
- replicate on a new sample.

A Bazi label that merely redescribes information already supplied to the reader earns no predictive credit.

---

## 8 · Event test design

For an event claim, preregister:
- exact event definition;
- time window;
- probability/confidence;
- competing baseline probability;
- what would count as failure;
- what model rule generated the prediction.

Example structure:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Target | job change |
| Window | next 12 months |
| Baseline | 18% from relevant cohort/history |
| Bazi prediction | 35% |
| Rule frozen | yes |
| Outcome | later recorded |
| Credit | based on calibration / discrimination, not story quality |

Broad statements such as “career energy changes around this period” receive no score until converted into a defined event.

---

## 9 · Bias controls

Bazi validation must actively guard against:
- Barnum/Forer-style broad descriptions;
- confirmation bias;
- hindsight bias;
- cherry-picking memorable hits;
- flexible time windows;
- changing schools after failure;
- using known biography while claiming the chart alone produced the insight;
- multiple-comparison fishing across many symbolic rules.

GUANSHI anti-hindsight rule applies fully: original prediction remains frozen after the outcome.

## 10 · Historical source anchors

### Chao Wei-pang, 1946
**The Chinese Science of Fate-Calculation**, *Folklore Studies* 5, 279–315.  
https://www.jstor.org/stable/3182937

Useful because the paper explicitly surveys:
- origin/history of fate calculation;
- Five Phases and stems/branches;
- Four-Pillars construction and luck cycles;
- pre-Song methods;
- Song/Yuan/Ming and later methods.

Historical source, not validation of predictive accuracy.

### Li Xuzhong mingshu reference
ChinaKnowledge / Ulrich Theobald.  
https://www.chinaknowledge.de/Literature/Daoists/lixuzhongmingshu.html

Useful for textual-layer caution: surviving material contains explicit hour/Four-Pillars references that may be later additions relative to the earliest Li Xuzhong tradition.

### Yuanhai Ziping
Chinese Text Project.  
https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?if=gb&remap=gb&res=727782

Traditional Zi Ping source. Attribution, dating and edition history require critical handling.

### Sanming Tonghui
Chinese Text Project, Ming text attributed to Wan Minying, Siku Quanshu base edition.  
https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?if=gb&remap=gb&res=532360

Preserves extensive material on stems/branches, seasons, year/month/day/hour, luck cycles and fate-calculation rules.

## 11 · Evidence labels

- Birth date/time/place: **Established factual input** if documented.
- Stem–branch conversion: **Established as a reproducible calendar calculation** under declared convention.
- Historical existence of Bazi/Ziping traditions: **Established historical fact** at the level supported by sources.
- Day Master / Ten Gods / strength / structure / useful-god framework: **Traditional**.
- Personality prediction: **Traditional / test required** unless independently supported for a defined target.
- Life-event prediction: **Traditional / Exploratory** until prospectively validated.
- Deterministic claims of inevitable fate: **Rejected as an operational GUANSHI standard**.

---

## 12 · T06 contribution to GUANSHI

T01–T05 built:

**Change → Relation → System → State → Time**

T06 adds:

**Person × Time**

Operationally:

**What is changing? → What is interacting? → What system is forming? → What state are we in? → Where are we in time? → What do we actually know about the person, and does the traditional time-pattern add information beyond that?**

This is the first GUANSHI node where a traditional personal model is explicitly forced to compete against an ordinary real-world baseline.
