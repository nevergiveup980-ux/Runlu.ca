# RUNLU GUANSHI · T02 阴阳 / Yin–Yang

Status: **V0.5 · deep dive**  
Public page: `guanshi-yinyang.html`

## Working definition

For GUANSHI, yin–yang is first treated as a **contextual relationship model** rather than a pair of permanent essences.

Operational questions:

1. What two tendencies are meaningfully contrasted?
2. Relative to whom / what / where / when?
3. Do the two sides define, constrain or enable each other?
4. Which tendency is waxing and which is waning?
5. Under what conditions could the relation reverse or transform?
6. What proportion or rhythm best supports the present goal and constraints?

This does **not** establish yin and yang as scientifically verified physical substances or forces.

## Source layer A · Received Yijing tradition

Primary anchor:
- `繫辭上 / Xi Ci I` contains the canonical formulation `一陰一陽之謂道`.
- Source: Chinese Text Project: https://ctext.org/book-of-changes/xi-ci-shang

GUANSHI use:
- document the traditional formulation;
- do not infer from its canonical status that a modern empirical claim has been proven.

Label: **Traditional + Symbolic**.

## Source layer B · Modern philosophical interpretation

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy · Metaphysics in Chinese Philosophy
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-metaphysics/

Useful point for GUANSHI:
- yin and yang can be understood as contextual categories or tendencies rather than intrinsic permanent properties;
- the same thing may be classified differently in different relationships.

This strongly supports GUANSHI's requirement that every yin/yang label specify a reference relationship and context.

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy · Philosophy in Han Dynasty China
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/han-dynasty/

Useful point for GUANSHI:
- yin and yang are used to interpret change through contrasting and alternating aspects;
- treating them simply as independent substances is historically and philosophically misleading.

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy · Chinese Philosophy and Chinese Medicine
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/chinese-phil-medicine/

Useful point for GUANSHI:
- yin–yang was applied broadly in later cosmological, medical and correlative systems;
- these later applications should be tracked by domain instead of collapsed into one universal empirical claim.

# Six operating relations

## YY01 · 对 / Contrast
Define the actual pair.

Bad use:
- force every complex system into two boxes.

Better use:
- identify a genuinely useful contrast, while allowing third variables and non-binary structures.

## YY02 · 位 / Context
Every label must answer:
- relative to whom / what?
- at what time?
- in what place / system?
- toward what goal?

Rule:
**No context, no yin/yang classification weight.**

## YY03 · 互 / Co-dependence
Ask whether the two tendencies:
- define each other;
- constrain each other;
- enable each other;
- compete for limited resources;
- stabilize each other.

Do not assume co-dependence automatically; identify the mechanism.

## YY04 · 消长 / Waxing and waning
Translate symbolic language into time-dependent observations where possible:
- trend direction;
- rate of change;
- duration;
- threshold;
- volatility.

## YY05 · 转 / Transformation
A reversal claim is incomplete without a trigger.

Required fields:
- present dominant tendency;
- hypothesized reversal trigger;
- observable warning indicator;
- expected time window;
- alternative explanation.

## YY06 · 和 / Dynamic coordination
Do not interpret balance as mandatory 50/50 symmetry.

Operational question:
**What mix, rhythm or alternation best supports robust functioning for this goal, stage and constraint set?**

# Translation protocol: symbol → variable

A yin/yang statement receives **no empirical decision weight** until translated.

Template:

1. **Traditional statement** — e.g. “yang is excessive; add yin.”
2. **Named tendencies** — what exactly counts as each side here?
3. **Observable indicators** — what can be measured or reliably observed?
4. **Time window** — over what period?
5. **Threshold / comparison** — relative to what baseline?
6. **Proposed intervention** — what is being changed?
7. **Outcome measure** — what result would count as improvement?
8. **Review** — did the intervention help, fail, or reveal a wrong framing?

If steps 2–7 cannot be completed, the statement remains **Symbolic / Exploratory**.

# Examples for future GUANSHI use

## Work / organization
Traditional prompt:
- too much outward expansion (yang), insufficient consolidation (yin).

Possible translation:
- new commitments per week;
- unfinished backlog;
- staffing capacity;
- error/rework rate;
- recovery / maintenance time.

## Personal schedule
Traditional prompt:
- activity/rest imbalance.

Possible translation:
- active hours;
- sleep duration;
- recovery periods;
- subjective fatigue;
- performance trend.

This is **not** a medical diagnostic rule. Health applications require domain-specific evidence and professional standards.

## Business strategy
Traditional prompt:
- expansion vs preservation.

Possible translation:
- growth spending;
- cash runway;
- customer acquisition;
- retention;
- operational capacity;
- downside exposure.

The yin/yang pair helps expose a neglected counter-force; financial conclusions must come from financial evidence.

---

# Red lines

GUANSHI must not:
- assign permanent yin/yang identity to people;
- turn yin/yang into gender stereotypes;
- claim a symbolic label is a measured physical force;
- use “balance” to avoid making a real trade-off;
- predict an outcome merely because a traditional correspondence exists;
- rewrite a failed prediction after the fact.
