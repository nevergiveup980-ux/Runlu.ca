Longevity and Healthy Life Expectancy: What's the Difference?

Living Longer Is Not Always Living Better

Life expectancy estimates average years of life in a population. Healthy life expectancy adjusts for time spent in different health states. Neither predicts the exact lifespan of an individual.

Healthy Years Do Not Mean Zero Diagnoses

WHO's healthy life expectancy measure (HALE) adjusts years for the severity of ill health; it does not simply count years without a diagnosis. A person managing a chronic condition may still have strong function and wellbeing.

Function Matters in Everyday Life

WHO defines healthy ageing around functional ability: being able to do what matters to us, such as moving, making decisions, caring for ourselves, maintaining relationships and contributing to society.

Environment Changes What Is Possible

Handrails, safe lighting, accessible transport and supportive relationships can make independence easier even when physical capacity is limited. Healthy ageing depends on people and their environments.

Longevity Is Not a Competition

Genes, illness, social conditions and access to care all influence health. No supplement, fasting schedule or exercise guarantees a particular lifespan. Chasing a number can create unnecessary anxiety.

Focus on What Can Be Improved

Physical activity, strength and balance, adequate food and sleep, vaccinations, chronic disease care, avoiding tobacco and meaningful social ties are practical areas to discuss with professionals.

Ask What You Hope to Keep Doing

Instead of predicting your age at death, consider the activities you want to preserve: walking, traveling, learning or spending time with loved ones. Healthy years matter because of what they allow us to do.

Today's practice

Write down three things you hope to do independently in five years, then choose one small, safe action toward one of them.
