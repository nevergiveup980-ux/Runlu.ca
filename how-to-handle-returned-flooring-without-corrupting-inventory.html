How to Handle Returned Flooring Without Corrupting Inventory

Returned material is not automatically available inventory. Before a roll, cut piece, carton or supply item goes back into stock, the warehouse needs to know exactly what came back, how much is usable, what condition it is in and where it will be stored.

Return-driven accuracyIdentify → Inspect → Measure → Classify → Locate → Restore

The return event should reconnect material to inventory without pretending that everything coming back is identical to what originally left.

RETURN IS A DECISION POINT

Returned does not mean available.

01

Identify what returned

Reconnect the material to the original roll, cut piece, carton product or supply record whenever that history is available.

02

Inspect condition

Separate usable material from damaged, contaminated, opened or otherwise questionable material before changing available inventory.

03

Measure what is actually usable

For returned carpet, verify the physical remaining length or piece size instead of restoring the quantity that originally left by assumption.

04

Classify the material

The result may return to normal inventory, become a remnant, remain pending inspection or follow another exception path.

05

Assign a real location

Restored inventory should have a physical warehouse location so the system does not create stock that nobody can find.

06

Preserve the return history

Keep the movement from warehouse to destination and back again visible so later cuts, transfers or adjustments have context.

SAFE RESTORATION

Restore only what the warehouse has verified.

The reliable rule is simple: a return can increase available inventory only after the returned material has been identified, inspected, measured or counted, classified and placed. That protects the inventory record from optimistic quantities that do not exist on the rack.
