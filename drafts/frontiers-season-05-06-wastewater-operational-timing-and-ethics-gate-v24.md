## Direct source checks
1. Québec 2025 evaluation, full text: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09603123.2025.2591162 ; PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41310418/
   - Median estimated infection-to-availability: 13.1 days wastewater vs 8 days clinical. Do not mislabel as a direct lab-processing comparison or a universal result.
   - Distinct logistical lead times: approximately 72.5 hours wastewater versus 31.4 hours clinical. These are different from the 13.1/8-day end-to-end intervals.
   - Clinical symptom-onset date available in only 27% of cases, limiting the comparison; asymptomatic infections complicate onset comparisons.
   - Cross-correlation patterns between wastewater and clinical indicators not consistent/interpretable across periods; results depend on sampling and reporting context.
   - Thus the claim 'wastewater invariably warns first' is not supportable.
2. WHO pilot WES guidance 6 December 2024, 74 pages, https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/wastewater-and-environmental-surveillance-for-one-or-more-pathogens--guidance-on-prioritization--implementation-and-integration
   - WES includes sewage population samples and human-impacted waters where sewer systems absent, as part of multimodal surveillance.
   - WHO PDF https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/wash-documents/wash-related-diseases/wes-for-one-or-more-pathogens--guidance-on-prioritization--implementation-and-integration--pilot-version6dec2024.pdf?download=true explicitly notes legal ownership, sample testing/storage/disposal, data rights and jurisdiction-specific privacy/law-enforcement separation. WHO identifies this as a pilot document; do not claim finalized ethical framework.
3. PHAC National Wastewater Monitoring technical notes https://health-infobase.canada.ca/wastewater/technical-notes.html
   - Respiratory activity levels combine site-relative signal level and trend, with at least 12 months of valid history for the index.
   - Geographical aggregation is population weighted, and site coverage matters.
   - Influenza A wastewater source can be human, animal or livestock: not automatically human cases.
   - Snowmelt, precipitation, industrial flow, assay and collection affect measurements.
   - The 95% uncertainty intervals for modeled Rt reflect model/input assumptions but exclude uncertainty in viral concentration measurement and environmental factors. Do not call these all-inclusive uncertainty bands.
4. PHAC overview https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/emergency-preparedness-response/wastewater-monitoring.html
   - Typically 3–4 weeks of consistent sampling and testing needed to identify meaningful trends; integrate with clinical surveillance.
   - Cannot identify individuals or reliably distinguish residents from travellers.

## Editorially useful new framing
A signal has multiple clocks: infection, shedding, sampling, lab receipt, PCR completion, quality control, publication and decision. The actionable question is not merely whether a signal exists, but when and for whom it becomes interpretable. Make the distinction between a *measurement*, an *indicator*, a *forecast*, and a *policy decision*.
A strong vignette could contrast a sample physically collected on Monday with a report that reaches public health after several additional steps; no invented dates, durations or fictional real-world case presented as observed.

## Specific safeguards
- Do not imply sewer-connected communities represent rural or unsewered residents.
- Do not infer prevalence, individual identity or diagnosis from a single community sample.
- Do not equate a dashboard update date with sample collection date.
- Do not turn Québec single-program median delays into a universal ranking of wastewater versus clinical testing.
- Distinguish WHO pilot guidance from binding regulations.
- Do not treat population-weighted aggregates as proof of full population coverage.
- Do not claim Rt model confidence intervals include every uncertainty source.
