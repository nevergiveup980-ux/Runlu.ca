## Central editorial proposition
Neural measurements can support limited, conditional inferences about aspects of cognition. These are not equivalent to unrestricted, accurate reading of another person's private thoughts. The public-interest question is how to preserve agency, consent and mental privacy as measurement and inference improve.

## Primary research and policy sources
1. Tang et al., 'Semantic reconstruction of continuous language from non-invasive brain recordings', Nature Neuroscience 26, 858–866 (2023), DOI 10.1038/s41593-023-01304-9. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01304-9
   - fMRI-based decoder reconstructs semantic gist of perceived or imagined language under research conditions.
   - The authors report that participant cooperation is necessary both for training and successful application. This is not unrestricted mind reading.
   - Limitations: participant-specific training, constrained laboratory setup, semantic approximations rather than verbatim transcripts.
2. UNESCO, Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology, adopted 11 November 2025. https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-ethics-neurotechnology
   - Paragraphs 5–6 distinguish neural data and indirect data enabling mental-state inferences.
   - Paragraphs 47–50 address freedom of thought, mental privacy, consent, data minimization and protection.
   - This is an international recommendation to member states; do not misrepresent as universally binding domestic law.
   - Paragraphs 125–130 discuss risks and safeguards in workplaces.

## Evidence-to-claim guardrails
SUPPORTED: Under specific experimental conditions, AI systems can infer some semantic information from neuroimaging data.
SUPPORTED: UNESCO recognizes neural data and inferred mental states as ethically sensitive, recommending protections for privacy and autonomy.
NOT SUPPORTED: Any commercial device can read anyone's private thoughts at will.
NOT SUPPORTED: The cited experiment recovered exact internal speech verbatim.
NOT SUPPORTED: The UNESCO recommendation itself automatically creates enforceable law in Canada or worldwide.
NEEDS FURTHER RESEARCH: Current consumer-device accuracy, clinical BCI capabilities, Canadian privacy legislation, workplace practices and cross-cultural perspectives.
