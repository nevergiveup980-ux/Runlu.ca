## Proposed topic
CN: 《城市污水里的健康信号》 (working)
EN: *What Can a City's Wastewater Tell Us?* (working)
Question: How can population wastewater sampling help public-health officials detect and interpret community pathogen circulation, and where does inference stop?

## Directly verified primary sources
1. WHO, 6 Dec 2024, *Wastewater and environmental surveillance for one or more pathogens: guidance on prioritization, implementation and integration*, PILOT version, 74 pages, https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/wastewater-and-environmental-surveillance-for-one-or-more-pathogens--guidance-on-prioritization--implementation-and-integration . WHO frames WES as population sampling from sewers or human-impacted waters, to be integrated with multimodal surveillance. This round checked the publication overview, **not the entire 74-page technical PDF**.
2. Public Health Agency of Canada, *About the dashboard*, https://health-infobase.canada.ca/wastewater/about.html . Surveillance covers COVID-19, flu, RSV, mpox; community and institutional pathogen signals; not identification of individual cases or households. PCR and weekly aggregates for several respiratory pathogens.
3. PHAC, *National Wastewater Monitoring of Pathogens*, https://health-infobase.canada.ca/wastewater/ . At retrieval on 2026-11-18, page reports last updated 2026-11-12, with data up to 2026-11-01; some stations lag one week or more. Do not equate update timestamp with sample date.
4. PHAC, *Technical notes*, https://health-infobase.canada.ca/wastewater/technical-notes.html . Snowmelt, rain, industrial flow, temperature, collection technique, variant mixtures and viral shedding can alter signal. PHAC cautions against using cross-wave wastewater signals to estimate case counts.
5. PHAC, *Wastewater monitoring*, https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/emergency-preparedness-response/wastewater-monitoring.html . Supports situational awareness alongside clinical testing; cannot identify individuals or determine if a signal came from residents or travellers.

## Claim control
SAFE: 'Wastewater monitoring may provide community-level situational awareness and sometimes early warning.'
UNSAFE without study-specific validation: 'Wastewater reliably predicts outbreaks X days ahead of clinical cases.'
SAFE: 'Some pathogens may be shed without symptoms; sampling captures community signal.'
UNSAFE: 'One sample determines infection prevalence, individual diagnoses, or exact number of cases.'
SAFE: 'Trends depend on assay, collection, catchment, weather, shedding, data processing, and reporting delays.'
UNSAFE: 'All sewer-connected and unsewered populations are represented equally.'
SAFE: 'PHAC's dashboard was updated Nov 12 2026, with a separate underlying sample-data cutoff.'
UNSAFE: 'Nov 12 means every station sampled on Nov 12.'

## Potential distinct narrative
Opening: A municipal wastewater sample is collected before individual patients are counted.
Science: shedding -> sewer catchment -> sample -> molecular assay -> normalized signal -> trend.
Use: augment clinical surveillance, guide additional testing and communication.
Limits: weather, representation, lead time, clinical confirmation, privacy and uncertainty.
Close: a city has biological signals but not a crystal ball.
