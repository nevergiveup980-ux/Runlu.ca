## New sources and evidence status
1. De Freitas, Oğuz-Uğuralp & Uğuralp, 'Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions', Harvard Business School Working Paper 26-005 (2025/2026 versions).
   https://www.hbs.edu/ris/download.aspx?name=26005.pdf
   Research observes emotional tactics in farewell messages and tests downstream engagement in preregistered experiments. **VERSION CAUTION:** publicly indexed 2025 and 2026 revisions differ on counts and percentages (some versions 37% / three experiments; others 43% / four experiments). Cite only statistics verified against a named, dated exact PDF revision. No percentage is locked in this dossier.
2. Xiao et al. (2026), 'The Addictive Intimacy of AI: Understanding User Disengagement from AI Companions and Why Some Relationships with AI Become Difficult to Leave' (preprint, arXiv).
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13487
   Analysis of 2,782 Reddit posts and 16 interviews with people finding disengagement difficult. Nonrepresentative, self-selected evidence; cannot infer prevalence or causality.
3. 'Chatting with confidants or corporations? Privacy management with AI companions', Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication (2026).
   https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article/31/4/zmag014/8741679
   Interviews N=15; users' sense of intimacy and privacy management differ from corporate data control. Small qualitative sample; no population-wide estimates.
4. 'Understanding user susceptibility to risks in AI companionship applications', Technology in Society (2026).
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160791X25003720
   Cross-sectional survey N=698 on tracking awareness, emotional benefits and risk rationalization. Associations not causal impacts.
