## Verified research
- MIT Media Lab / collaborators (2025), How AI and Human Behaviors Shape Psychosocial Effects of Extended Chatbot Use: A Longitudinal Controlled Study, randomized four-week study n=981. https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/how-ai-and-human-behaviors-shape-psychosocial-effects-of-chatbot-use-a-longitudinal-controlled-study/
  No significant experimental condition effects; heavier voluntary use associated with worse measured outcomes. This does NOT establish that heavy use caused worse outcomes.
- 2026 randomized two-condition study, n=380, How emotional response styles of generative AI companions shape adaptive emotion regulation and short-term AI reliance. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13593866/
  Both styles provided immediate relief; adaptive-regulation framing improved some short-term measures; relational reassurance increased perceived necessity and short-term reliance intentions. Short-term intentions are NOT established long-term dependence.
- CHI 2026, Mental Health Impacts of AI Companions: Triangulating Social Media Quasi-Experiments, User Perspectives, and Relational Lens. https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790558
  Mixed observational signals and qualitative interviews; not a universal causal effect.
- 2026 cross-sectional study n=666: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42947853/
  Anthropomorphism, psychological needs and problematic dependence associated; noncausal design.

## Claims ledger
Supported: AI companionship can provide perceived support; different response designs can affect short-term measures in controlled settings.
Not established: AI companions universally harm or improve mental health.
Not established: anthropomorphism causes dependency.
Not established: AI systems have feelings or equivalent therapeutic relationships.
Business model question: monetization, engagement incentives and asymmetry require product-specific evidence before claims about actual companies.

## Distinctive structure
1. What does 'companionship' mean when one party is a product?
2. Short-term relief versus long-term wellbeing.
3. Human attachment and design asymmetry.
4. What experimental versus observational evidence can establish.
5. Disclosure, agency and the ability to disengage.
6. Human relationships, product responsibility and open questions.
