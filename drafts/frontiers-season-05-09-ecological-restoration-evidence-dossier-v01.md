## Working title
当修复自然也可能带来新风险
When Restoring Nature Creates New Risks

## Specific question
Why can well-intentioned ecological interventions cause unexpected ecosystem changes, and what safeguards can improve restoration outcomes?

## Evidence anchors
1. Parks Canada, Principles and Guidelines for Ecological Restoration in Canada's Protected Natural Areas: https://parks.canada.ca/nature/science/conservation/ie-ei/re-er/pag-pel
   Guidance cautions against assuming invasive species control alone ensures ecosystem recovery and recommends assessing negative species interactions, genetic diversity and natural regeneration.
2. Society for Ecological Restoration, International principles and standards for the practice of ecological restoration, third edition (2026): https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/rec.70441
   Restoration may stimulate undesirable species; monitoring, adaptive management and cautious genetic supplementation are needed.
3. Evaluating unintended consequences of intentional species introductions and eradications for improved conservation management (2022), global literature review of 111 case studies: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9291768/
   Distinguish sample of reported cases from representative global incidence; do not extrapolate a percentage of all projects.
4. IUCN invasive species planning and management on islands: https://www.cpsg.org/sites/default/files/2024-05/IUCN%2520Guidelines%2520for%2520invasive%2520species%2520planning%2520and%2520management%2520on%2520islands.pdf
   Follow-up restoration and monitoring needed after eradication; unintended invasions can occur.

## Claim controls
Supported: ecological restoration can produce unintended consequences; design should consider species interactions, genetics and monitoring.
Avoid: 'restoration generally harms nature' or the claim that all introductions are invasive.
Avoid: treating a literature sample as global project failure rate.
Avoid: depicting assisted migration as always good or always harmful.
Avoid: implying any intervention outcome is known with certainty.

## Outline
1. Why good intentions are not an ecological mechanism.
2. An ecosystem is a network of interacting organisms and processes.
3. When removal of one species creates a new opening.
4. The genetic and ecological risks of reintroductions.
5. Baselines, field trials, monitoring and adaptive management.
6. Restoration as careful stewardship, not a one-time symbolic gesture.
