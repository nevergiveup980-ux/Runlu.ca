## Candidate
记忆可以改变，但我们还是自己吗？
If Memory Changes, Are We Still Ourselves?

## Verified literature
- Elsey, van Ast & Kindt (2018), Psychological Bulletin, Human memory reconsolidation: A guiding framework and critical review of the evidence. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29792441/
  Reports evidence consistent with reconsolidation but inconsistent findings and alternative explanations prevent conclusive neurobiological inference.
- Brunswick et al. (2025), Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, Not the same as it ever was: A review of memory modification, updating, and distortion in humans and rodents. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763425001952
  Memory is reconstructive; modification may be adaptive or maladaptive.
- Review of reactivation-induced memory updating (2022): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35247380/
  Replication problems and context sensitivity remain.

## Evidence boundaries
Supported: memory can be updated and distorted; retrieval and reconsolidation are studied in humans and animals.
Unsettled: reliable and selective manipulation of complex autobiographical memory in everyday humans.
Not supported: a clinically available, precise 'delete a chosen memory' capability.
Philosophical inference, not experimental fact: what changing memory means for personal identity.

## Distinctive structure
1. Memory as reconstruction, not video playback.
2. Retrieval, updating and competing mechanisms.
3. Why replicability matters.
4. Autobiographical memory versus lab tasks.
5. Personal identity as a philosophical question.
6. Respecting the person while acknowledging scientific uncertainty.
