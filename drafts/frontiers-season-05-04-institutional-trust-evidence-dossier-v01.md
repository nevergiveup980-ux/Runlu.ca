## Working title
信任从何处破裂，又如何修复？
How Trust Breaks—and How It Can Be Rebuilt

## Distinctive empirical question
What factors are associated with trust in public institutions, especially perceived voice, responsiveness, reliability, integrity, openness and fairness? Do not generalize to interpersonal trust.

## Evidence
- OECD, Survey on Drivers of Trust in Public Institutions 2026 Results, executive summary: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-survey-on-drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-2026-results_9eb63fec-en/full-report/executive-summary_4319a992.html
  2025 wave, 33 OECD countries and five accession candidates; OECD average 40% high/moderately high trust in national government, 43% low/no trust. OECD averages are not global population proportions. Political voice trust gap reported as 47 percentage points; this is an association, not causal effect.
- OECD 2026, socioeconomic conditions, political agency and trust: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2026/06/results-of-the-2025-oecd-survey-on-drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions_96323a65/full-report/socioeconomic-conditions-political-agency-and-trust_bb385ad9.html
  About 31% say people like them have a say in government. Distinguish this from specific country's percentage.
- OECD 2026 Canada country note: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-survey-on-drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-2026-results_c88c8869-en/canada_44908581-en.html
  Canadian positive perception of use of evidence 49% (2023) -> 54% (2025); political voice 40% (2023) -> 44% (2025). Do not imply that the change caused increased trust.
- OECD 2024 detailed driver model: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-survey-on-drivers-of-trust-in-public-institutions-2024-results_9a20554b-en/full-report/the-public-governance-drivers-and-personal-characteristics-shaping-trust-in-public-institutions_b27d32e4.html
  Association between trust and perceived evidence-based decision-making, intergenerational fairness, political voice; modeled percentage-point differences are not randomized intervention effects.

## Claims ledger
SUPPORTED: trust differs across institutions and countries; political voice and perceptions of governance are associated with institutional trust.
NOT SUPPORTED: listening to citizens always restores trust; OECD survey proves causation; trust is identical to government approval; all societies are losing trust.
LIMITATION: cross-country survey instruments and modeled associations need contextual interpretation.

## Six-part outline (not article prose)
1. Why public institutions need trust without demanding unquestioning loyalty.
2. Different objects of trust: national government, local services, courts, police.
3. Voice and responsiveness: being heard versus being obeyed.
4. Fairness, integrity, reliability and transparency as perceptions and practices.
5. What surveys reveal and cannot establish about repair.
6. Trust as a relationship maintained through accountable action.

## Distinguish from Essay 10
Essay 04: survey-based public trust, legitimacy, citizen voice and perceived responsiveness.
Essay 10: formal policy evaluation, assigned corrective actions and institutional feedback loops. Do not turn 04 into a policy evaluation manual, or 10 into trust-restoration rhetoric.
