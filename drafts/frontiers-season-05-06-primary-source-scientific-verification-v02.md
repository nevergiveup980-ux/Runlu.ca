## 1. Québec programme evaluation — VERIFIED WITH PUBLIC FULL-TEXT SOURCE
Title: *Tracking SARS-CoV-2 transmission: evaluation of the Québec wastewater surveillance program*
Authors: C. Jobin, M. Dubé, F. Bouchard, F. Lamothe, L. Duquesne, C. Huot.
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/09603123.2025.2591162
Full text: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09603123.2025.2591162
PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41310418/
Publication: published online **27 November 2025**; journal issue **2026**, volume 36 issue 7, pp. 1687–1701.
Study samples: March 2022–March 2023, first programme year, Québec. Municipal WWTP influent 24-hour composite samples, 3–7 samples weekly depending on site. The programme covered up to 48% of provincial population via 19 sites in 14 cities and 13 health regions.
Findings: median estimated infection-to-availability delay **13.1 days** for wastewater data versus **8 days** for clinical data. Study defines timeliness to include disease-related time, logistical delay, and dissemination to public health decision makers. **Wastewater dissemination was identified as the main bottleneck.** Variation across sites was substantial. Clinical and wastewater trends aligned during some periods, not all; cross-correlation did not yield consistent interpretable patterns.
Safe narrative wording (English): “An evaluation of Québec's programme, published online in November 2025, estimated median infection-to-data-availability intervals of 13.1 days for wastewater results and 8 days for clinical results during the programme's first year (March 2022–March 2023). The researchers identified dissemination delays as a major obstacle and warned that results varied by site. The figures do not establish a universal ranking of surveillance methods.”
Do not generalize from this to all pathogens, settings or periods. Avoid using clinical onset missingness statistics without checking their precise basis.

## 2. Alberta longitudinal methods comparison — VERIFIED
Title: *Consistent trends from different methods for monitoring SARS-CoV-2 in urban wastewater during a 29-month longitudinal study*
DOI: https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1547831
Full text: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1547831/full
Study: **1,482** samples, **five wastewater treatment plants**, **three Alberta cities**, monitoring **up to 29 months**. Compared two molecular processing workflows; agreement with each other and 5-day rolling averages of clinically diagnosed COVID-19 cases, particularly early when routine clinical testing was available.
Safe narrative wording: “A 29-month Alberta methods study analysed 1,482 samples from five plants across three cities. Two analytical workflows showed consistent trends and, during periods of routine clinical testing, correlated with reported case trends. This supports trend monitoring, not a claim of universal lead time.”
Important local nuance: Calgary's studied municipal system separates stormwater runoff from the wastewater inflow. Therefore **do not imply the Alberta study demonstrated direct stormwater dilution at those plants**. General weather/runoff caveats may remain as general WES limitations, not as a finding specific to those plants.
