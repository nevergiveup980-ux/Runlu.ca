## Important correction to v2.4
The WHO 2024 PILOT guidance described a WES-specific ethics framework as under development. **That status is outdated as of 2025-07-18**. WHO published *Ethics framework for wastewater and environmental surveillance* on 18 July 2025: https://pandemichub.who.int/publications/9789240116412 . Future articles must cite the published 2025 framework, not repeat the earlier 'still being developed' statement.

WHO's 2025 framework lists seven ethical assessment areas: programme aims; potential public-health benefits; potential harms and mitigation; proportionality; equity and justice; respect for persons; data and sample stewardship. It highlights privacy, stigma, community engagement, transparency, data sharing and trust. The 2024 pilot guidance remains useful for prioritization and feasibility but is not the latest WES ethics authority.

## New 2026 global context
WHO, *Wastewater and environmental surveillance — a global landscape analysis*, published 18 June 2026, 104 pages: https://pandemichub.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-011279-7 . The overview reports global interest alongside challenges of governance, data use, ethics and long-term sustainability. Value requires alignment with national priorities, integration into decision-making, clear institutional leadership and sustainable financing. This round inspected the WHO publication overview, not all 104 pages.

## Scientific and editorial synthesis
- 2024 WHO pilot prioritization: public-health significance -> feasibility -> acceptability -> optimization.
- 2025 WHO ethics framework: independent ethical criteria beyond the laboratory.
- 2026 WHO landscape analysis: governance and financing for long-term operational utility.
- PHAC technical notes: population-level indicators, not diagnoses; wastewater signals require interpretation and have sampling/environmental limitations. https://health-infobase.canada.ca/wastewater/technical-notes.html
- Québec 2025 program evaluation (see v2.3/v2.4) demonstrates a detected signal is not necessarily available earlier than clinical reports.

Suggested thesis: *A city can detect a biological signal, but turning it into useful and fair public-health action requires timely measurement, context, and accountable governance.*

## Evidence boundaries
No claim that WHO frameworks are binding law; no claim that any one pathogen, jurisdiction or sampling site has universally superior early warning. Do not imply full WHO PDF review when only the published abstracts/selected passages were checked. Distinguish current authoritative ethics framework from superseded prospective wording.
