## Strong empirical case: Québec program evaluation (2025)
Study: *Tracking SARS-CoV-2 transmission: evaluation of the Québec wastewater surveillance program*, DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/09603123.2025.2591162 ; published online 2025-11-27, program launched March 2022 and evaluated after first year.
Directly checked article abstract and accessible PDF excerpts. Findings:
- Temporal agreement between wastewater and clinical indicators varied by period.
- Cross-correlation did not provide consistent interpretable wastewater/clinical timing patterns.
- Unplanned missing wastewater results ranged 1–21%, median 3%.
- **Median estimated interval from infection to wastewater data availability: 13.1 days, compared with 8 days for clinical data.** These are estimated availability intervals, not simple sample-to-result lab turnaround or measured outbreak lead time.
- Data dissemination delay was the principal bottleneck for timely public-health availability.
- Clinical symptom-onset date recorded for only 27% of reported cases; clinical delay estimate therefore has limitations.
- Laboratory and shipping times varied by city, laboratory and sampling day.
**Editorial implication:** Avoid portraying wastewater surveillance as invariably earlier than clinical reports. A measured population signal may arrive too late to provide operational early warning.

## Contrast: Alberta methodological study (2025)
*Consistent trends from different methods for monitoring SARS-CoV-2 in urban wastewater during a 29-month longitudinal study*, Frontiers in Microbiology, DOI https://doi.org/10.3389/fmicb.2025.1547831 .
Study used five treatment plants in three Alberta cities, 1,482 wastewater samples across up to 29 months. Two workflows (affinity columns + RT-qPCR DNA standards versus ultrafiltration + RT-qPCR RNA standards) correlated well with one another and with five-day moving averages of clinical COVID-19 cases when routine clinical testing existed.
**Editorial implication:** Evidence for useful tracking and reproducibility across workflows does not establish an invariant early lead over clinical data. Need examine full study methods/limitations before precise correlation claims.

## Further cautionary empirical case (2024)
Li et al., *Temporal assessment of SARS-CoV-2 detection in wastewater and its epidemiological implications in COVID-19 case dynamics*, Heliyon 10(8):e29462, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e29462 ; PMC https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11024598/
Abstract: No wastewater lead during Delta or post-Omicron phase, lead seen in Omicron phase, possibly affected by overwhelmed clinical testing, reporting delays or shedding patterns.
**Editorial implication:** The same monitoring approach can have different apparent lead times across phases; do not infer universal forecasting power.

## Science explanation to verify
'Signal present in collected sewage' and 'signal available to public-health staff' are separate dates. End-to-end operational value requires timely sampling, shipment, laboratory processing, quality control, interpretation and dissemination. Also compare like-for-like reference indicators: case reports, percent positive, hospital admissions and infection onset are different clocks.

## Proposed narrative shift
Not 'A city can predict outbreaks from sewage', but 'A city can measure a collective biological signal; whether that becomes an early warning depends on measurement, reporting speed and what it is compared against.'
This is more distinctive and better supported than a sensational fixed-days-ahead claim.
