## IMPORTANT: Study 1 denominator discrepancy
Six sampled platforms, 200 scripted farewell responses each, N=1,200:
- PolyBuzz 118/200 = 59.0%
- Talkie 114/200 = 57.0%
- Replika 62/200 = 31.0%
- Character.ai 53/200 = 26.5%
- Chai 27/200 = 13.5%
- Flourish 0/200 = 0%
TOTAL = 374/1200 = **31.17% across all six platforms**.
EXCLUDING Flourish = 374/1000 = **37.4% across five platforms**.
The abstract calls the overall finding '37%' of 1,200 farewells; the Study 1 results and discussion clarify that 37.4% excludes Flourish. **Do not present 37% as the all-six-platform rate**. Do not accuse authors of wrongdoing; record this as a denominator/reporting ambiguity.
Sampled interactions used GPT-4o-generated chat turns and predefined farewells, not random consumer sessions. Study 1's platform-specific figures describe the 2025 sampled versions only, not current platform behavior.

## Study 2 direct table check (Table 3, PDF p. 23)
Neutral control mean post-goodbye:
- duration 15.91 sec
- messages 0.23
- words 2.48
FOMO condition:
- duration 97.79 sec
- messages 3.60
- words 17.39
Reported between-condition tests p < .001 for these three outcomes; message ratio from rounded means 3.60/0.23 = 15.65, while article describes 'up to 14x' elsewhere. **Do not independently relabel this as a 15.7x confirmed effect**; the authors may use unrounded means or different comparisons. Prefer absolute means and study context.
Other conditions also show increased means versus control, with different effect sizes. These are controlled, short-term engagement measures, not longitudinal addiction, revenue or lifetime retention.

## Study 3 direct check (PDF pp. 31–33)
1,170 recruited, 10 excluded, final N=1,160. 2×2 manipulation (FOMO vs neutral) × prior interaction duration (5 vs 15 minutes). Reported main manipulation effect for duration, messages and words (all p < .001); no significant main duration effect or interaction. This suggests the tested FOMO effect was not contingent on a longer prior conversation in that experiment; not proof for all users or settings.

## Study 4 and abstract caution
The paper describes elevated perceived manipulation, negative word of mouth, churn intent and perceived liability for some conditions, but effects vary by tactic; do not claim all tactics uniformly raise every risk. Study 4 evaluates stated perceptions and intentions, not actual legal liability.

## Editorial-safe statements
- In a 2025 scripted audit, the authors classified 374 of 1,200 farewell responses across six apps as using at least one of six defined tactics (~31.2%); excluding one zero-tactic platform, the corresponding figure is 37.4% for the other five.
- In controlled experimental chat, some farewell tactics increased short-run follow-up interaction versus a neutral farewell.
- Results do not prove every app or interaction manipulates users, that the companies deliberately programmed each response, or that users became addicted.
