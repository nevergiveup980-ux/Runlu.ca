## Working title
看似坚固的文明为何会失灵？
Why Do Resilient Societies Fail?

## Specific question
How do interdependencies among electricity, communications, transport, fuel and water turn a local disruption into a cascading service failure?

## Evidence anchors
1. NIST, Community Resilience Planning Guide for Buildings and Infrastructure Systems, Volume II (SP 1190v2), section on dependencies and cascading effects: https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2017/05/09/NIST-SP-1190v2.pdf
   Case: 2003 Northeast blackout, internal grid cascade and cross-sector consequences; document estimates about 50 million people affected in US and Canada.
2. Public Safety Canada, Renewing Canada's Approach to Critical Infrastructure Resilience: What We Heard Report: https://www.securitepublique.gc.ca/cnt/rsrcs/pblctns/rnwng-cnd-pprch-crtcl-nfrstrctr-rslnc-2022/index-en.aspx
   Consulted stakeholders flagged interdependencies and cascading impacts as areas needing more risk analysis. This is consultation evidence, not a measured prevalence rate.
3. NIST resilience draft on interdependencies and cascading effects: https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/el/building_materials/resilience/Disaster_Resilience_Chapter_4_Interdependencies_Cascading-Effects_50-Draft_102014.pdf
   Explicitly distinguish this draft from final guidance.

## Claim controls
Supported: interdependent infrastructure can produce within-system and cross-system cascades.
Supported: 2003 blackout offers a documented historical example.
Avoid: a single software bug alone caused the entire blackout; NIST depicts interacting conditions.
Avoid: 'modern civilization is inevitably collapsing' or all cascades follow the same sequence.
Avoid: claiming consultation responses quantify all Canadian infrastructure risks.

## Outline
1. A power outage that reached beyond electricity.
2. What a dependency is, including shared dependencies.
3. The 2003 blackout as a bounded case.
4. Cascading versus common-cause versus escalating failures.
5. Isolation, redundancy, restoration order and tradeoffs.
6. Resilience as measurable service recovery, not a claim of invulnerability.
