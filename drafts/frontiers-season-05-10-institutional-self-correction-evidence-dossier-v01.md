## Working title
当文明学会重新审视自己
When Civilization Learns to Question Itself

## Narrow, defensible question
What institutional arrangements allow societies to discover mistakes, evaluate interventions and change course rather than merely announce that lessons were learned?

## Primary evidence
1. OECD Council Recommendation on Public Policy Evaluation (2022), OECD/LEGAL/0478: https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0478
   Recommends institutionalised evaluations, quality controls, integration of results in policy/budget decisions, public findings by default, and assigned follow-up.
2. OECD, Implementation Toolkit (2025): https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/2025/02/implementation-toolkit-for-the-oecd-recommendation-on-public-policy-evaluation_f24516be.html
   Practical tools for embedding policy evaluation and learning; recommendation is guidance, not proof of effectiveness in every setting.
3. OECD, Government at a Glance 2025, §5.2: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/government-at-a-glance-2025_0efd0bcd-en/full-report/public-policy-evaluation_e59d50bb.html
   In 2023 OECD survey (31 responding countries): 26 had central coordinating institutions; 19 published evaluation results by default; only 7 had ministry-level follow-up mechanisms for all evaluations. Denominators apply ONLY to responding countries and surveyed institutional features.

## Editorial thesis
A civilization's capacity for self-correction is not an abstract virtue but a set of observable feedback loops: define goals, measure effects, expose findings to scrutiny, assign decisions, implement changes, and test again.

## Claims ledger
Supported: evaluation and follow-up mechanisms are recommended international public-governance practices; implementation varies substantially.
Not supported: OECD survey proves that evaluation causes trust or prosperity.
Not supported: all countries follow OECD recommendations.
Not supported: a published evaluation necessarily results in corrective action.
Avoid: conflating policy evaluation with moral virtue, AI self-improvement or general 'humanity awakening'.

## Six-part outline
1. The difference between admitting error and changing course.
2. What a policy evaluation measures, and what it cannot establish.
3. Independent scrutiny and public access to findings.
4. Why evaluation reports often fail to change decisions.
5. Closing the loop: owners, timelines, implementation, remeasurement.
6. A society able to revise its decisions without losing its principles.
