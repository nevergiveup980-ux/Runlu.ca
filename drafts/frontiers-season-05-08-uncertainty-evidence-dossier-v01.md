## Candidate title
当科学不能给出确定答案
When Science Cannot Give a Certain Answer

## Core question
How should people make consequential decisions when evidence is incomplete, likelihoods are uncertain, and some uncertainty cannot be reduced quickly?

## Independent primary and peer-reviewed evidence
1. IPCC AR6 WG1, Chapter 1, Box 1.1: calibrated confidence (quality/amount/consistency of evidence and agreement) is distinct from likelihood (probabilistic uncertainty). The guidance acknowledges communication can be misunderstood.
https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/chapter-1/
2. Government of Canada, Canada's Changing Climate Report 2026, Chapter 1 §1.4.3: explicitly explains confidence vs likelihood and calibrated uncertainty.
https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/science-technology/changing-climate-report-2026/cccr-chapter-1-en.html
3. van der Bles et al., PNAS 2020, 'The effects of communicating uncertainty on public trust in facts and numbers': five experiments including a preregistered replication and field experiment, total n=5,780; communicating epistemic uncertainty led to only small trust decreases in the tested settings, mainly with verbal rather than numeric expressions. Do not generalize to all populations, topics, or uncertainty types.
https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/117/14/7672.full.pdf
4. National Academies, Communicating Science Effectively: A Research Agenda (2017), chapters on uncertainty and science controversies; stresses beliefs, values, interests and context.
https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/23674/chapter/4
