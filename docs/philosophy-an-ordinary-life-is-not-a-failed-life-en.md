# An Ordinary Life Is Not a Failed Life

**RUNLU STORIES · PHILOSOPHY IN EVERYDAY LIFE · 05**

**RUNLU · Original essay**

## 1. When Did We Begin to Fear Being Ordinary?

When we were children, adults liked to ask what we wanted to be when we grew up.

Some of us wanted to become scientists, others doctors, others people whose names would be known around the world. Few children would say they hoped to become ordinary people who worked conscientiously and lived well.

It was almost as though becoming someone remarkable were the only way to make a life worthwhile.

Then we grew up. Most of us did not become the people we had imagined. Instead, we began our lives on city streets, on factory floors, behind shop counters, and in ordinary offices.

We learned to earn a living, to carry responsibilities, and to get up on time the morning after an exhausting day.

Why, then, is such a life so often regarded as less than successful?

## 2. A Life Measured in Numbers

Modern society has grown increasingly accustomed to describing people in numbers.

How much they earn, how large their homes are, how high they have risen at work, how much wealth they possess, and how many people follow them.

Numbers make comparisons easy. They also make it easy to forget that some things cannot be measured in numbers at all.

A father sitting awake beside a sick child, a mother caring for her family year after year, a worker taking care to do every job properly—these things rarely appear in rankings of successful people.

Yet if we removed such acts from society, how much of the prosperity we celebrate would remain?

Numbers can tell us something about what a person owns. They cannot fully tell us what that person has given to the world.

## 3. The Work That Receives No Applause

Early in the morning, before the city is fully awake, some people are already at work.

Someone is cleaning the streets. Someone is sorting deliveries. Someone is preparing breakfast. Someone else is finishing a night shift.

Their names will not appear in the news, and few people will stop to applaud them.

Yet it is precisely this seemingly unremarkable work that allows everyday life to run smoothly.

The world advances through the extraordinary inventions of a few, but it also depends on the care and persistence of countless ordinary people.

The absence of applause is not the absence of a contribution.

Some work matters precisely because nobody notices it: someone quietly dealt with a problem before it had a chance to become one.

## 4. The Dignity of Ordinary Work

Occupations differ, and so do incomes. But neither difference should determine the dignity of a human being.

A person who cleans a room with care and a person who designs a building with care both create value through their labour.

The dignity of work lies not simply in a job title, but in the care with which the work is done and the respect and fair compensation every worker deserves.

Working conscientiously does not mean accepting unfair treatment. Workers have every right to seek better pay, safer conditions, and greater opportunities.

Respecting ordinary work and striving for a better life have never been contradictory.

We can hope for a better tomorrow without looking down on the work we do today.

## 5. Responsibility Is an Achievement in Itself

When we are young, we often think of achievement as doing something others have not done.

As we grow older, we begin to discover that some achievements have little to do with surpassing others. They lie in continuing to honour our responsibilities as the years go by.

Raising children, accompanying parents as they grow old, keeping a household going, and doing what we can to protect the people we love when difficulties arise.

There is no shared graduation ceremony for these things, no certificate declaring that we have succeeded.

They are made of countless ordinary days, with their exhaustion, disagreements, frustrations, and unexpected moments of happiness.

Taking responsibility does not require us to give up every part of ourselves.

But making room in our lives to care for others, while also caring for ourselves, is an achievement worth cherishing.

## 6. We Need Not Become Someone Else's Idea of Success

Sometimes we become so concerned with proving that we are living well that we forget how to live well.

We buy things we do not need to impress others, accept lives we do not enjoy to win approval, or hurry down roads that do not suit us for fear of falling behind our peers.

Other people see only fragments of our lives. We are the ones who must live through every day.

Work that suits us, a relationship in which we can speak honestly, and time we can call our own may be more precious than an enviable life that leaves us exhausted.

We need not reject conventional success. Nor must we treat it as the only valid answer to the question of how to live.

Life is not a race in which everyone must reach the same finish line.

## 7. Being Ordinary Does Not Mean We Stop Growing

Accepting that we are ordinary does not mean giving up our dreams.

An ordinary person can still learn, discover new interests, create something of their own, or begin an entirely new journey in the second half of life.

Growth need not have the ambition of changing the world.

Learning a new skill, understanding a book that once seemed beyond us, fulfilling a long-held wish, or becoming a little better at living alongside others can all be forms of growth.

Not everything we create needs a market to establish its worth. Not everything we love needs to earn an income to justify its place in our lives.

When we are no longer in a hurry to prove ourselves to others, we may find more courage to try the things we genuinely enjoy.

An ordinary life need not be where dreams end. It can be the soil in which they take root.

## 8. Living With Care

Many years from now, when we look back on the roads we have travelled, we may find that the moments we miss most are not always those in which we received recognition.

Perhaps it will be an evening around the family table, a sunset glimpsed on the way home from work, or a small project finally completed after many unsuccessful attempts.

Perhaps it will simply be an ordinary morning when we woke up and still had something to look forward to.

The meaning of life need not lie hidden at some distant destination.

It is also found in the things we have done with care, the people we have treated with sincerity, and the days we have not carelessly let slip away.

We may never be remembered by our age, but we can live a life we ourselves will be glad to remember.

An ordinary life is not a failed life.

**To work conscientiously, treat others with sincerity, keep our passions alive, and live well in the time we have is an achievement in itself.**
