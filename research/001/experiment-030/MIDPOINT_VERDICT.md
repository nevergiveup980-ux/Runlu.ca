# Experiment 030 — Research 001 Midpoint Verdict

## Question
After E001–E029, what has actually survived?

## Verdict on the original classical fallback-correlation hypothesis
The broad idea that pre-agreed/shared classical correlation may improve no-communication warehouse fallback behavior over naive independent decisions is trivially possible against weak baselines.

But once the comparator is strengthened to the full relevant classical set—deterministic local policies, arbitrary shared-classical mixtures, fixed roles, and pre-agreed schedules—Research 001 has not demonstrated a correlation-specific operational advantage.

E020 proves that the E004 shared-bit family is already inside the randomized-classical convex hull.
E023 shows the first blind-corner native objective is too weak: its classical hull fills the feasible metric simplex.
E025 finds a mathematical limitation only after adding TIGHT-priority, but E026 rejects that interpretation as an unvalidated warehouse safety rule.
E027 makes safety invariant and exposes strong fixed/alternating classical schedules.
E028 identifies local queue pressure as a defensible private efficiency variable.
E029 then shows the remaining limitation is missing private information: shared randomness coordinates roles but does not reveal the other side's current queue state.

## Midpoint decision
**Close the classical "special correlation advantage" branch as unsupported in the tested warehouse-native models.**

This is not a proof that no future warehouse task can ever benefit from shared correlation. It is a decision that the present research program has no justified basis for claiming a special advantage after matched classical controls.

## What survives
A stronger research question:

**What is the minimum timely information and trust structure required for safe, efficient distributed coordination when communication degrades?**

This question is supported independently by the E009–E017 communication/trust line and the E028–E029 queue-pressure line.
