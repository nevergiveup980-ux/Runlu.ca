## Purpose

GUANSHI is an open decision framework that combines:

1. evidence-based modern decision methods;
2. direct observation of real conditions and constraints;
3. traditional Chinese models of change, time, person and environment as cultural/heuristic lenses;
4. long-term recording, review and correction against real outcomes.

It is **not** presented as a scientifically proven fortune-telling system.

## Core rule

> If a traditional claim conflicts with reliable evidence, evidence wins.

Chinese: **任何传统理论一旦与可靠事实冲突，以事实为准。**

A second rule now accompanies it:

> Tradition may suggest a lens. Science determines what may be claimed. Practice decides what survives.

Chinese: **传统可以提供观察视角；科学决定什么可以被声称；实践决定什么最终留下。**

## Four-layer evidence structure

### 1. Science
Probability, decision theory, Bayesian updating, behavioral science, systems thinking, scenario analysis, robustness and risk management.

### 2. Reality
Current conditions, resources, constraints, history, environment, observable behavior and external conditions. Reality is the primary input, not an after-the-fact explanation.

### 3. Tradition
Yijing, yin-yang, five phases, bagua, stems and branches, Bazi, divination methods, feng shui and related systems are treated as interpretive, cultural or heuristic models unless an individual claim has independent empirical support.

### 4. Validation
Predictions and recommendations should be recorded before outcomes are known. Repeated failures reduce a model's weight. Useful signals earn further testing, not automatic promotion to fact.

## Nine lenses

- **易 — Change**: what is emerging, declining, turning or transforming?
- **象 — Pattern**: what recurring structure or relationship can be observed?
- **数 — Measure**: what can be quantified, compared or assigned uncertainty?
- **时 — Time**: what is the relevant cycle, horizon and decision timing?
- **地 — Environment**: how do place, layout, access and external conditions shape the choice?
- **人 — Person**: what goals, abilities, habits, biases, resources and limits matter?
- **事 — Situation**: what concrete decision is actually being made?
- **变 — Scenarios**: how may the situation change under plausible futures?
- **势 — Dynamics**: after integrating the above, which direction has the strongest evidence-adjusted momentum?

## V0.3 knowledge map

### Traditional learning / dependency line

**Yijing → Yin–Yang → Five Phases → Bagua → Stems & Branches → Bazi → Six Lines / Meihua → Feng Shui**

This is a **learning and model-dependency sequence, not a strict historical genealogy**. Stems/branches are historically very old; bagua belongs within the Yijing tradition; yin–yang and Five Phases also developed through broader and partly parallel intellectual histories. Source history is recorded separately from learning order.

1. **Yijing / change philosophy** — change, position, relation, timing and transformation.
2. **Yin–Yang** — relational vocabulary for contrast, alternation and balance; not assumed to be a physical force.
3. **Five Phases** — historical cycle/relationship model; metaphors separated from causal claims.
4. **Bagua** — pattern-classification lens; predictive power not assumed.
5. **Stems, Branches & calendrical cycles** — calendar facts separated from later symbolic/personality/destiny claims.
6. **Bazi / Four Pillars** — historical person × calendrical-time pattern system; deterministic claims require prospective testing.
7. **Six Lines / Meihua** — later event-focused divination traditions studied separately; attribution and textual layers preserved.
8. **Feng Shui / environment** — measurable environmental variables explicitly separated from symbolic rules.

The detailed source spine is maintained in `GUANSHI_TRADITIONAL_LINE.md` and published as `guanshi-tradition.html`.

### Modern decision line
1. **Probability & base rates** — replace absolutes with ranges and uncertainty.
2. **Bayesian updating** — revise confidence as evidence changes.
3. **Decision trees** — expose alternatives, consequences and trade-offs.
4. **Behavioral science** — check predictable cognitive biases.
5. **Risk & robustness** — favor choices that survive wrong assumptions, especially under irreversible downside.
6. **Systems thinking** — model feedback, delay, bottlenecks and second-order effects.
7. **Scenario analysis** — compare plausible futures instead of claiming one certain future.
8. **Decision journal & calibration** — record predictions before outcomes and measure later accuracy/confidence.

### Convergence rule

Traditional lenses + observed reality + decision science + outcome review → **势 / integrated direction**.

GUANSHI does not ask only, “What is my fixed fate?” It asks:

- What is happening now?
- What is changing?
- Which forces matter?
- What can be measured?
- What remains uncertain?
- Which action is robust?
- What actually happened after we acted?

## Evidence labels

Every substantive GUANSHI claim should eventually carry one of these labels:

- **Established** — supported by strong, relevant evidence.
- **Supported** — useful evidence exists but limits remain.
- **Exploratory** — plausible hypothesis or early signal.
- **Traditional** — preserved from a historical/traditional system; scientific support not established.
- **Symbolic** — cultural/philosophical interpretation, not an empirical fact.
- **Rejected** — contradicted by reliable evidence or repeatedly failed validation.

Mixed topics may require multiple labels. Example: a feng shui discussion can contain measurable environmental claims and symbolic traditional claims in the same case; they must be separated rather than blended.

## Traditional source protocol

Each traditional node now targets four distinct records:

1. **Primary text** — what a canonical or historically important text actually says.
2. **Textual history** — date, attribution, layers, later commentary and uncertainty.
3. **Modern scholarly history** — how historians and scholars describe the system's formation and use.
4. **Empirical claim audit** — where a measurable claim exists, what credible modern evidence supports or contradicts it.

A source may establish that an idea was historically taught. It does not by itself establish that the idea predicts modern outcomes.

## Decision loop

**Observe → Frame → Estimate → Decide → Record → Review → Reweight**

Each stored decision should support:

- facts available at the time;
- missing information;
- assumptions;
- alternatives considered;
- base rates / priors where available;
- modern model inputs;
- traditional model inputs, if used;
- evidence label for each traditional input;
- scenarios and downside;
- confidence / uncertainty;
- recommendation;
- strongest counterargument;
- falsification conditions;
- actual outcome;
- later review;
- model-weight adjustment.

The canonical preregistration structure is in `GUANSHI_CASE_TEMPLATE.md`.

## Anti-hindsight protocol

A prediction should be recorded before the outcome is known. After the outcome, the original entry must remain unchanged. Reviews are appended with dates.

This prevents three common failures:

1. vague predictions being reinterpreted after the event;
2. remembering hits while forgetting misses;
3. changing the original explanation to fit the result.

## Weighting principles

V0.3 does **not** assign arbitrary universal percentages such as “science 40%, tradition 10%.” Weight depends on the decision and evidence available.

Instead:

- reliable direct evidence outranks symbolic interpretation;
- strong domain base rates outrank unsupported intuition;
- reversible low-cost experiments may justify more exploratory inputs;
- irreversible/high-downside choices require stronger evidence and more conservative thresholds;
- a traditional model can gain research attention only through prospectively recorded, repeatable added value;
- a model that merely redescribes facts already known receives no predictive credit;
- repeated failure leads to lower weight or rejection.

## Boundaries

GUANSHI must not use divination or traditional symbolism as the deciding basis for high-stakes medical, legal, financial, safety or mental-health decisions. It may help organize uncertainty and questions, but it does not replace qualified professional advice.

For high-stakes domains, traditional lenses may be discussed as cultural/symbolic context only; evidence-based and professional methods control the recommendation.

All four public languages — English, Simplified Chinese, French and Spanish — must be complete before a GUANSHI public page is considered publishable under RUNLU's multilingual policy.
