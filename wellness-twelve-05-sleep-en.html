Sleep: The Body's Most Underrated Recovery Process

Sleep is not wasted time

Sleep is not a shutdown. While we rest, the brain remains active and the body continues regulating hormones, immune function and metabolism. Inadequate sleep can affect attention, mood and everyday safety.

The stages of sleep

Sleep cycles through non-rapid-eye-movement and rapid-eye-movement stages. These stages contribute to memory processing and emotional regulation. Sleep is important, but it is not a miracle cure for every illness.

How much is enough?

Most adults generally need around seven to nine hours a night, though individual needs vary. Quality, consistency and daytime functioning matter too. Persistent fatigue or signs of sleep apnea deserve medical assessment.

Tradition and the body clock

Many traditions value a regular rhythm of work and rest. Modern science studies how light affects circadian rhythms. Daylight exposure and consistent routines can help, while shift workers and caregivers may need tailored approaches.

Small changes tonight

Make enough time for sleep, reduce bright light before bedtime and consider the timing of caffeine. For persistent insomnia, ask a qualified professional about evidence-based approaches such as cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I). The aim is sustainable recovery, not perfect sleep.

RUNLU · Wellness Practice

  • Protect a realistic sleep window.
  • Allow quiet wind-down time before bed.
  • Seek help for persistent insomnia or severe daytime sleepiness.

General health education, not individualized medical advice.

Further reading: American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) and Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) adult sleep guidance.
