## Editorial positioning
An accessible, intellectually serious nonfiction short book exploring the historical Chinese idea of 天人相应 and its dialogue with modern life science. Philosophy, historical description, established scientific findings, and tentative hypotheses must be distinguished explicitly. Not a medical treatment guide.

**Chinese master target:** approximately 40,000 Chinese characters, flexible 35,000–50,000 according to editorial merit; do not pad.
**English edition:** original Western-context nonfiction adaptation preserving the conceptual architecture and scientific qualifications; not word-for-word translation.
**Publishing:** one volume, digital EPUB, Apple Books intended after manuscript and metadata review. Website article remains free and independent.

## Content architecture
- Narrative opening grounded in ordinary lived experience, not an invented personal anecdote presented as fact.
- Historical concept: precise classical quotations with verified edition and chapter.
- Scientific evidence: distinguish mechanism, association, trial outcomes and recommendations.
- Human dimension: family, care, shift work, social determinants, and unequal access to healthy conditions.
- Practical closing: feasible, non-prescriptive reflection or exercise with cautions.
- Avoid false equivalence: qi ≠ electricity, meridians ≠ proven nerves, traditional organ-hours ≠ established detoxification timetable.
- Avoid overgeneralizing traditional Chinese medicine or implying that a philosophy alone validates a clinical intervention.
- Keep explanatory transitions and a consistent authorial voice; remove redundancy before expansion.

## Research and safety ledger
1. Classical quotation: 《素问·上古天真论》 “法于阴阳，和于术数，食饮有节，起居有常，不妄作劳” — verify authoritative edition and punctuation before print.
2. Circadian timing and light: US NHLBI, NIH, CDC/NIOSH, contemporary peer-reviewed reviews; confirm specific claims against sources.
3. Air quality and climate: WHO and relevant Canadian public-health agencies; differentiate hazard, exposure and outcome.
4. Activity and sleep: WHO physical activity recommendations and sleep medicine guidance; avoid one-size-fits-all schedules.
5. Social determinants: WHO and Canadian public-health evidence; avoid blaming individuals for structural constraints.
6. Mental health: established clinical guidance; never attribute all disease to emotions or discourage treatment.
7. Include bibliography, endnotes, terminology notes and a clear medical-information disclaimer.
