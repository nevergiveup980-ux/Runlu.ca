Protein: The Body’s Everyday Repair Work

1

Protein is not just for athletes. Every day, the body breaks down and builds proteins used in muscles, enzymes, transport and immune functions. Food supplies amino acids, but eating protein does not automatically build muscle.

2

For healthy adults, the North American Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day. This is a reference for meeting most healthy adults’ needs, not a universal optimum for training, aging or illness.

3

Eggs, fish, dairy, poultry, legumes, tofu, nuts and seeds can all contribute protein. Plant-based diets can meet needs with sufficient variety and total intake, while attention to nutrients such as vitamin B12 remains important.

4

Maintaining strength as we age involves adequate food, suitable resistance exercise, movement and recovery. Some expert groups suggest higher protein targets for older adults, but these do not apply to everyone, especially people with kidney disease or other medical restrictions.

5

Protein powder is convenient for some people, but it is not essential when food meets their needs. More is not always better, especially if high-protein choices displace vegetables, whole grains and other nutritious foods.

RUNLU · Practice

Consider whether each day's meals contain suitable protein sources. Build a varied routine that fits your appetite, culture and health rather than chasing a single number.

General education only, not personalized medical advice. Seek clinical guidance for kidney disease, pregnancy, breastfeeding, malnutrition or other special conditions.
