In the final stretch before graduation, the most common subject on campus was no longer coursework. It was where everyone was going next.

Some already knew where they would be sent; others were still waiting. Conversations in the dormitories gradually shifted from exams, teachers, and classmates to work units, cities, wages, and the future.

Zhang Yuansheng was waiting for his next page too.

At the time, graduate assignments were not simply a matter of each student being handed a notice. Students within the same major faced a list of available work units in an order shaped by academic standing and ranking.

Zhang Yuansheng’s grades were slightly better than those of one female classmate in the same major.

He happened to rank exactly one place ahead of her.

Among the available units, the Drilling Group Company was considered one of the better destinations. Under the normal order, when Zhang Yuansheng’s turn came, he could have chosen it first.

Before the choice was finalized, the classmate came to speak with him.

She felt that the unit she was likely to receive was not ideal and hoped Zhang Yuansheng would leave the Drilling Group Company opportunity to her.

It was not the kind of decision anyone expected to debate for days. They had studied together. One person asked; the other thought about it.

Zhang Yuansheng agreed.

The better opportunity that had been available to him was left for her, and he ultimately went to Shengli Oilfield Drilling Company No. 3.

At least in Zhang Yuansheng’s later memory, no word of thanks ever came.

At the time, he did not dwell on it. Everyone graduating was busy entering a new life. Who would imagine that a choice of work unit would return to memory years later?

After he had been working for a while, some university classmates gathered and, as usual, asked how everyone was doing.

Someone mentioned the female classmate.

Only then did Zhang Yuansheng unexpectedly learn, according to the other classmates, that she had never reported to the Drilling Group Company at all.

Her father held a position at a design institute. She had been waiting for him to arrange a way for her to return to the institute, and in the end that was the path she took.

Zhang Yuansheng paused when he heard it.

It was not that he suddenly believed anyone owed him something.

It was simply the first time he understood in a very concrete way that the opportunity he had seriously given up had never actually been something the other person intended to use.

Had he known, of course he would have kept the Drilling Group Company opportunity for himself.

But the choice had already been made, and he had already reported to Drilling Company No. 3. There was no way to rerun the order.

He did not go to argue with her.

Even many years later, though, the memory still carried a small sense of regret.

It had originally been a better choice that belonged to him.

In July 1994, after graduating from university, he formally began work in Shengli Oilfield.

His destination was not Oilfield Construction Company No. 3.

It was Shengli Oilfield Drilling Company No. 3.

That distinction was clear in his actual work history, though years later it was easy for memory to compress the period into a single sentence: after graduation, he went back to work in the oilfield.

A real life is never only one sentence.

There is always the part in between.

The first thing that changed when he stopped being a student and became an employee was time. School moved by semesters; work had no summer or winter break to divide the year for you. When you arrived, whether the job was finished, and whether someone else was still waiting for your part began to matter.

He was paid at the trainee rate.

But wages were wages. The first time a young person earns money through his own work, the idea of “making a living” suddenly becomes concrete.

His parents began to look at him a little differently too. However old a child is, as long as he is still in school, some part of him remains a student. Once he started working, the family began to ask his opinion on certain matters, and relatives stopped asking only about his studies.

His time at Drilling Company No. 3 lasted about a year and a half.

As for the exact position he held or every task he performed, Zhang Yuansheng had no wish, decades later, to invent a perfect résumé just to make the story look complete.

What remained was the feeling formed by entering a work unit for the first time: work was no longer like finishing a problem and handing it to a teacher. Whatever you did was connected to someone else’s next step.

A young employee gradually learns that being late, overlooking something, failing to listen carefully, or not following a matter through is no longer only his own problem.

He was not the kind of newcomer who became conspicuous on the first day.

More often, he watched first and listened first. He remembered what he did not understand. When something truly landed in his hands, he tried to see it through.

That quiet way of working stayed with him for a long time.

Many years later, in a warehouse in Canada, when inventory did not match, goods could not be found, or transfer records became confused, his instinct was still to ask: where did the problem begin, and how could it be prevented next time?

He could not have known then that decades later he would build that habit into a system called Warehouse OS.

The world visible to him in 1994 was not yet that large.

For now there was Shengli Oilfield, Drilling Company No. 3, a first paycheck, and a young man who had just left the classroom learning how to become an employee.

Canada was little more than a name on a map.

The next change to arrive was not a distant country.

About a year and a half later, another piece of paper carried his working life toward a different unit inside the oilfield.

This time, the name on the paper was Oilfield Construction Company No. 3.
