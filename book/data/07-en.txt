By the time Zhang Yuansheng truly entered Oilfield Construction Company No. 3, he was no longer a young man stepping straight out of the university gate.

About a year and a half at Drilling Company No. 3 had already worn away some of his student habits.

He knew by then that work inside a unit did not become easier simply because a person was young.

Once a task truly landed in your hands, the important question was often not “Do I know how to do this?” but “Did this finally get done?”

Those two questions sound similar, but they are separated by a great distance.

“Do I know how?” is mostly about yourself.

“Did it get done?” involves other people.

Work at Oilfield Construction Company No. 3 had a strong sense of the field: projects, equipment, vehicles, materials, people, one link connected to another. On paper, many matters could be written in a few lines; in real work, someone had to watch, ask, move, and connect the pieces.

As for the exact positions Zhang Yuansheng held or every site he worked at, there was no need decades later to invent details just to make the novel look complete.

What mattered was that those years slowly wrote a way of working into his character.

When a supervisor assigned something, it was not enough to hear only the general idea. When a colleague pointed out a problem, it could not simply be ignored because it belonged to someone else’s section. When something went wrong, taking hold of the problem first was often more useful than rushing to prove whose fault it was.

Zhang Yuansheng was not the kind of young employee who was best at making himself visible.

His strengths took longer to show.

Watch first. Listen first. Remember what you do not understand. When your turn comes, work through it piece by piece until it is done.

Over time, that is often how other people decide whether someone can be relied upon.

Not by how much he says, but by whether a matter keeps moving after it reaches his hands.

That habit followed him a very long way.

More than twenty years later, in a flooring-materials warehouse in Canada, when he dealt with receiving, inventory, transfers, cutting, and shipping, he still tended to see each step as part of one connected process.

Later still, when he built Warehouse OS together with artificial intelligence, he was simply turning an old habit into a system.

Of course, the years at Oilfield Construction Company No. 3 were not only about work.

A young person’s life grows in several directions at once.

Wages gradually stopped feeling like the novelty of “earning money for the first time” and became part of ordinary life.

His parents were still nearby.

His friends were still nearby.

And outside of work, another person was slowly entering his life.

In 1997, he married Lin Lan.

In 1998, their daughter Xiaoqi was born.

From then on, the question “How is work?” no longer concerned only one person. Behind every decision, another question naturally appeared: what about the family?

That is why Oilfield Construction Company No. 3 remained in his memory as more than a work unit.

It overlapped with the years of marriage, fatherhood, and a family gradually taking shape.

If life had continued along that road, it would have made perfect sense.

There was a stable work unit, a home, a child, and parents living within the same world. For an ordinary person, that was already a complete life path.

Canada was still far away.

But distance does not remain on a map forever simply because a person has not planned for it.

Later, the idea of going out to see another place, of trying another kind of life, began to appear.

At first those thoughts were so light that they hardly seemed like decisions.

By the spring of 2003, however, they had become something the family had to face for real.

In May 2003, Zhang Yuansheng ended this period of work at Oilfield Construction Company No. 3 and emigrated to Canada with his family.

From graduating in July 1994 and first going to Drilling Company No. 3, to transferring about a year and a half later into Oilfield Construction Company No. 3, and finally leaving in May 2003, nearly nine years of oilfield working life formed one complete line.

Looking back, he would understand how important that line had been.

While living through it, however, he was simply going to work one day at a time and living one day at a time.

A real life rarely tells you in advance which days will later become chapter titles.

Before the story reaches that departure, another person still has to enter it.

She is already close.
