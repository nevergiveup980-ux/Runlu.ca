Wood’s Homes: More Than a Century of Never Giving Up

Care begins with someone saying yes

In 1914, Reverend George Wood began caring for children in Innisfail, Alberta. From those early beginnings, Wood’s Homes grew into a nonprofit mental health organization that supports children, young people, adults and families. Its story reminds us that a lasting institution can begin with one human decision to help.

More than one kind of support

Today Wood’s Homes offers more than 40 programs and services. Its work includes counselling, therapeutic campus care, foster care, housing and hub services, and learning centres. Rather than assuming every person needs the same answer, the organization describes a family-centred approach that responds to different circumstances and needs.

A community is measured by how it responds

Mental health challenges can affect any family. Some people need a conversation; others need sustained treatment, safety, education or a stable place to stay. Organizations such as Wood’s Homes help make those forms of support part of community life. Their work is often quiet, demanding and difficult to capture in a photograph or headline.

Never giving up is a daily practice

“We Never Give Up” is the organization’s own guiding phrase. Its significance is not that every challenge has an easy solution. It is the choice to keep showing up for people whose paths may be complicated. Behind every program are staff, caregivers, volunteers and community supporters doing the patient work of care.
