RUNLU CHRONICLE 010 · 12 OCT 2026

One Thanksgiving Long Weekend, Three Breakthroughs, One New Record

The promise at the start of the weekend

At the start of Canada’s Thanksgiving long weekend, the plan was simple: finish one book and move one app through submission. By 11 October, the CSU research robot’s connection milestone had joined them. By 12 October, the record had grown again: the book had an Apple Books listing, and the game had received review approval.

One book reaches readers

THE GLASS THRONE is JOHN ZHANG’s English corporate suspense novel, presented by RUNLU as one complete book of sixty chapters. Its official Apple Books page now provides a place to preview and buy it. The submission remains part of its history; the Books archive now records the subsequent publication milestone.

One game, a fourth consecutive first-submission approval

RUNLU SHIFT passed on its first submission, marking RUNLU’s fourth consecutive first-submission approval. The elapsed time from submission to the review-completion notification was 18 hours 11 minutes, the fastest in this four-approval sequence.

The submitted V1.0 contains forty levels across two worlds. That defines the version behind this milestone: a concrete game with its own product, support and privacy pages. The achievement is worth celebrating precisely, without moving its release date forward on paper.

18 hours 11 minutes: what the new record measures

This interval includes the wait after submission; it is not a measurement of active reviewer time. Review approval is recorded here as its own milestone. An official App Store release has not been confirmed in this record.

One connection, more work ahead

On 11 October, RUNLU recorded the CSU-focused personal research robot’s connection milestone. It remains a development prototype within RUNLU Market Companion. A successful connection is a useful step, while reliable operation still needs repeated validation. One-to-five-minute market updates and stock discovery remain areas for further verification.

Any candidate the robot finds must go through further research and human review before a decision. This milestone makes no claim of autonomous trading.

What we carry into the next ordinary day

The most lasting part of a long weekend may be what survives when the celebration is over. A book can be opened by a reader. A game has passed a real review. A research tool has taken another practical step. Three different kinds of work have each moved beyond intention.

The new time record belongs in the Chronicle, but it is not a promise that every future review will be as quick. What we can carry forward is the habit behind this weekend: finish carefully, describe progress honestly, and return to the work. Gratitude is also the willingness to care for what we have begun.
