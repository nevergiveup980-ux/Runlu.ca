# RK-0007 — Why Your Business Can Be Busy but Still Run Out of Cash

**RUNLU KNOWLEDGE · Editorial brief · 2026-11-18**

## Reader problem
A small business may have a full schedule and show an accounting profit yet lack cash to pay payroll, suppliers or taxes when due. Explain the difference between sales, accounting profit and cash timing in plain language, without presenting one-size-fits-all financial advice.

## Reader outcome
By the end, a reader can distinguish revenue, profit, cash collected and available cash; identify payment timing gaps; build a simple rolling cash calendar; and recognize when to consult a qualified accountant or tax professional.

## Proposed structure
1. Opening scene: a cleaning contractor with a full weekly schedule and an unpaid invoice.
2. Four different numbers: invoiced revenue, earned profit, bank balance and cash available after upcoming obligations.
3. The timing trap: invoice terms, payroll timing, supplies and recurring overhead.
4. A transparent hypothetical: a profitable month with a temporary cash shortfall; explicitly label assumptions and separate tax treatment.
5. Cash calendar: opening balance + expected receipts − expected payments = projected balance, with cautious treatment of uncertain receipts.
6. Practical controls: invoice promptly, track overdue payments, schedule known bills, maintain a cash buffer where feasible, and avoid confusing tax money with spendable funds.
7. When a busy business is not profitable: distinguish low margins from a timing mismatch.
8. A one-page checklist and a disclaimer about jurisdiction, taxes, accounting method and professional advice.
