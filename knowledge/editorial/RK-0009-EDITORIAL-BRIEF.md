# RK-0009 — Editorial brief
Status: proposed, unpublished. 2026-11-27.
Working title: Why More Customers Can Leave a Business Owner More Exhausted
Chinese: 为什么客户越来越多，自己却越来越累？

## Reader and purpose
Small service business owners. Help distinguish demand growth from capacity growth, measure owner workload, and decide whether to simplify, reschedule, delegate or decline additional work. Not medical, employment or individual financial advice.

## Original structure
1. More customers do not create more hours.
2. Count delivery, travel, coordination, administration and rework without double counting.
3. Identify the actual bottleneck.
4. Compare workload before and after growth.
5. Reduce avoidable coordination and travel; preserve service quality.
6. Delegate only with clear scope, training and realistic costs.
7. Set a capacity limit and protect recovery time.
8. Seven-day workload log and weekly decision checklist.

## Fictional worked example
Before: 24 delivery + 6 travel + 5 administration + 3 rework = 38 owner hours/week. After: 30 + 9 + 7 + 4 = 50 hours/week. If the owner chooses a 40-hour planning ceiling, gap = 10 hours. This is a fictional planning constraint, not a legal or medical standard. Example improvements: save 3 travel hours, 2 administration hours, 1 rework hour; transfer 4 delivery hours to a trained colleague, net of any additional owner supervision (explicitly model it). No promise of savings; test actual results. Workload reduction is not equivalent to profit improvement.
