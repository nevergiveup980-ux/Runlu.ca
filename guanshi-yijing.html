RUNLU GUANSHI · V0.4 · T01

Yijing · The Grammar of Change

Text first · interpretation labeled · prediction tested

The first question is not “What does the hexagram predict?”

It is: what layer of the Yijing are we reading, what structure does it represent, what can change, when, and from which position?

第一问，不是“这一卦会预测什么？”

而是：我们读的是《易》的哪一层？它描述什么结构？什么可以变？何时变？从什么位置去变？

La première question n’est pas : « Que prédit cet hexagramme ? »

Elle est : quelle couche du Yijing lisons-nous, quelle structure représente-t-elle, qu’est-ce qui peut changer, quand et depuis quelle position ?

La primera pregunta no es: «¿Qué predice este hexagrama?»

Es: ¿qué capa del Yijing estamos leyendo, qué estructura representa, qué puede cambiar, cuándo y desde qué posición?

For GUANSHI, the Yijing is first a disciplined vocabulary of state, relation, timing, position and transition — not a license for certainty.

01

Textual layers

Do not flatten the whole Yijing into one voice.

Core Zhouyi

64 hexagram configurations/names, hexagram statements and line statements form the older textual core commonly distinguished by modern scholarship.

Canonical commentaries

Later writings traditionally grouped as the Ten Wings expand the philosophical language of change, image, line relations, timing and human action.

Later schools

Xiangshu, Yili and technical divination traditions add further systems. GUANSHI records them as later layers instead of projecting them backward into the oldest core.

05

Boundary

What survives the first cut.

Keep

  • Change as the central object of attention
  • State and transition thinking
  • Position-sensitive interpretation
  • Timing-sensitive action
  • Image as hypothesis generation
  • Reproducible procedures as testable objects

Quarantine / reject

  • Deterministic 'this hexagram guarantees X' claims
  • Projecting later systems into the oldest Zhouyi
  • Post-hoc reinterpretation after outcomes are known
  • Symbolic correspondence presented as physical causation
  • Traditional numerology presented as statistics
  • Rules so vague they can explain every outcome
