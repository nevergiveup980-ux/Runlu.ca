## Editorial premise / 立意

The second season addressed food and nutrition, while the third developed movement, recovery and sleep. Season Four explores everyday **emotional wellbeing, stress and supportive relationships**, with attention to the interactions of social conditions, mind and body. It is **public health education, not diagnosis, psychotherapy or a promise of cure**.

核心思想：**情绪不是必须战胜的敌人，韧性也不是永远硬撑。** 让读者知道哪些反应属于正常生活，怎样寻找适合自己的支持；当问题持续、恶化或影响安全时，不应以自我调节代替专业帮助。

## Recommended six-part progression

- **01–03 基础 / Foundations:** Recognize the stress response, the role of emotions and why persistent stress deserves attention. Separate normal emotion from clinical conditions.
- **04–06 工具 / Skills:** Notice personal patterns; discuss relaxation and mindfulness cautiously, without claiming a single technique works for all or replaces care.
- **07–09 环境与关系 / Context and connections:** Address workplace and caregiving pressures, social connection and uncertainty, including external constraints and inequity.
- **10–12 支持与收束 / Resilience and care:** Define resilience without glorifying overwork; explain when and where to seek help; close with autonomy, dignity and sustainable ways of living.

## The lecture acceptance checklist / 每讲验收标准

1. Four **complete, independently readable** articles in ZH, EN, FR and ES, with natural and accurate culturally appropriate language. No placeholders or machine-like literal transliteration.
2. Distinct editorial perspective, meaningful introduction, **six substantive sections with at least two explanatory paragraphs each**, three realistic low-risk actions, an explicit safety/limitations note, and verified primary-source reading links.
3. Distinguish stress from clinical anxiety/depression; avoid diagnosing readers, definitive claims about cortisol, unproven mental 'detox', guarantees about breathing/mindfulness, and dismissing social or workplace problems as individual attitude.
4. For distress that is persistent, functionally impairing or otherwise serious, recommend qualified evaluation. Avoid generic crisis hotline numbers being presented as valid in every country; localize urgent advice and link to local emergency options when known. Immediate danger calls for local emergency help.
5. Mind-body practices should be optional adjuncts with mixed/condition-dependent evidence; trauma-related or other adverse responses can occur and professional guidance may be needed. Do not encourage abruptly stopping medication.
6. Cite original institutional sources such as WHO, CDC and NIH/NCCIH; confirm active URLs and fact accuracy at each lecture's publication date. **Write original prose**; do not translate or reproduce protected articles.
7. Use the established 4-way hreflang and canonical conventions, previous-lecture navigation, localized article links, phone-friendly display and return to wellness.html. For Lecture 01 use a return-to-season-index link rather than a nonexistent Lecture 00.
8. GitHub branch → four files → updated index → automated source QA → PR review and merge → user iPhone acceptance before treating each lecture as fully accepted. Season archiving only once 12 lectures are complete and accepted.
